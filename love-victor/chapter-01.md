# Chapter 01: Ground Level

*(POV: Victor)*

The thing nobody tells you about Ferris wheels is that they don't stop when the moment's over.

Benji and I had just said everything at the top, and then the car jerked and started going down again. Neither of us talked on the way. Up there it had felt like we were the only two people in Atlanta. By the time we hit the bottom, we very much weren't.

The operator swung the bar open. "All right, lovebirds. Out."

"We're not..." I started, and then stopped, because we kind of were.

Benji stepped out first. Then he turned around and held his hand out to me, and I took it.

"VICTOR SALAZAR."

Felix was pushing through the line, with Mia and Andrew behind him and Lake and Lucy behind them.

"We saw," Felix said. "Lake goes, 'Isn't that Benji getting on the Ferris wheel?' and I go, 'No way, Benji's on a plane to Connecticut,' and she goes, 'Felix, I know what Benji looks like.'" He looked at our hands and his whole face went soft. "Dude."

"Yeah," I said.

"*Dude*."

"I know."

He hugged me hard. Over his shoulder I saw Mia with both hands over her mouth.

"I thought you left," she said to Benji.

"I almost did."

She hugged me, and then Benji too, which I don't think he expected.

"Happy for you, man," Andrew said, and bumped Benji's fist. Then he nodded at me. "That speech today was pretty good, by the way."

"Pretty good?" Mia said.

"It was great. Don't tell him I said great."

Lake had her phone halfway out. Lucy put a hand over it without looking.

"I wasn't going to post it," Lake said.

"You were going to post it."

"*Tastefully*." Lake smiled at me. "Congrats, Victor. Seriously."

"Okay, I hate to be the sad one again," Mia said, "but I have one week left. One. So all of you are hanging out with me every single day until I leave. That's not a request."

"I'll make a schedule," Lake said.

"Please don't make a schedule," Lucy said.

"I'm making a schedule."

Felix leaned in close. "Your family's by the funnel cake stand. Your mom was asking where you went. I didn't say anything. Figured it was your story to tell."

"Thanks, Felix."

He looked at Benji. "Good to have you back, man." Then he followed the others toward the games, and it was just us again.

"Walk with me?" Benji said. "My mom's in the parking lot."

---

"So your mom drove you," I said. "The same mom who was standing next to your dad at the car two hours ago."

"The same one." Benji put his hands in his pockets. "After you left, we sat in the driveway for a really long time. Then we sat in the kitchen for a really long time."

"And?"

"And I told them I'm not going to Loomis Chaffee. I'm staying at Creekwood. I'm going to keep going to meetings and keep calling Dale and do all of it. But I'm doing it here."

I stopped walking. "You're staying."

"I'm staying."

I'd been so focused on the wheel, on *room for one more*, that I hadn't let myself think about what came after. Connecticut had felt like a door slamming. Now it just wasn't there.

"What did your dad say?"

Benji was quiet for a second. "A lot. Not much of it was good. I'm not going to tell you all of it. Some of it's between me and him." He looked at me. "But he knows it's my choice. And my mom's the one who offered to drive me here. So. That's something."

"That's a lot of something."

"Yeah." He almost smiled. "It is."

At the edge of the lot he stopped and turned to face me.

"I meant everything I said up there," he said. "But I need you to hear the other part too. I don't think you heard it."

"I heard it."

"You heard 'I want us to be together.' I need you to hear 'I still have to work on myself.'" He took a breath. "I can't do big things right now. No big declarations, no grand plans. I have to take this one day at a time, because that's how I stay sober. And I can't make you the thing that holds me together. If I do, and something goes wrong between us, I don't know what happens to me. Does that make sense?"

It did, and it didn't. Everything in me wanted to say *I'll hold you together, that's what I'm here for*. Because that's what I do. Victor's the strong one. Victor's the rock.

But I thought about what his dad had told me. *You're the reason he relapsed*. And I thought maybe this was Benji's way of saying: *you're not, and you don't get to be the reason I don't, either*.

"Slow," I said.

"Slow."

"I can do slow."

He raised an eyebrow. "Can you?"

"No," I said. "Probably not. But I can try."

That got a real laugh out of him. "That's honest, at least."

"I'm working on honest."

"Me too."

A car at the end of the row flashed its headlights.

"That's my mom's way of saying hurry up," Benji said.

"Should I say hi?"

He thought about it. "Yeah. I think you should."

The window rolled down. Benji's mom looked at me like she'd been crying earlier and had fixed it before we got there.

"Hi, Mrs. Campbell."

"Victor." It wasn't cold. It was careful, like she was holding something heavy and didn't want to drop it in front of me. "Benji told me about your speech. It sounded very nice."

"Oh. Thanks."

"It's late," she said. "He's had a long day."

"Right. Of course."

Benji squeezed my hand and let go. Before he got in, he looked at me over the roof of the car.

"Tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow."

I stood in the lot after they left, because I needed a minute. *I'm staying*. I said it once more in my head, just to hear it again.

---

My family was at a picnic table by the funnel cake stand. Mom and Dad were on the same side, shoulder to shoulder, laughing at something on Mom's phone. Pilar was texting. Adrian was eating.

"Mijo! There you are," Mom said. "Where did you run off to?"

I sat down across from them. I didn't know how to start, so I just started.

"Benji's staying," I said. "He's not going to Connecticut. He came to the carnival. We rode the Ferris wheel."

Pilar looked up from her phone.

"Hold on," Dad said. "This afternoon you said it didn't work out."

"It didn't," I said. "And then it did."

"So the dramatic sprint worked," Pilar said. "Eventually."

Mom said, "Ay, Victor," and came around the table and hugged me from behind, her chin on top of my head. Then she sat down next to me instead of going back to her side. She was smiling, but there was something else in her face too. She knew what this year had been like for Benji. She knew what it had been like for me.

"And how is he?" she asked. "Is he okay?"

"He's working on it. He said we have to go slow. That his recovery comes first."

"Good," Dad said, and he meant it. "That's a smart kid."

"Slow is good," Mom said. "Slow is very good. I'm proud of you, mi amor. Twice in one day."

"Okay, you guys are gonna make me cry at a carnival."

"You already made Dad sob," Pilar said. "It's only fair."

Dad pointed at her. "I had something in my eye."

"For the whole speech?"

---

At home, Pilar showed up in my doorway while I was getting ready for bed.

"So," she said. "Benji."

"Benji."

"Are you happy?"

"Yeah. And scared. Mostly both."

"That sounds like you." She came in and sat on the end of my bed.

"Can I ask you something?" I said. "You and Felix. I saw you pull him into the family picture."

She picked at the comforter. "He apologized. Like, a real apology, not a Felix speech. We're not back together. We're just... not broken anymore. He was family before he was my boyfriend. I don't want to lose that part."

"That's really mature."

"Don't make it weird."

"I'm not. I'm just saying you're growing up."

"And you're turning into Mom." She stood up. At the door she stopped. "For what it's worth, I'm glad he didn't leave."

"Me too."

---

I didn't sleep. I lay in the dark with my phone on my chest and kept replaying the wheel. *That isn't our story. This is*.

At 12:14, my phone buzzed.

**Benji:** Home. Called Dale. Told him everything. He says hi, which is weird because he's never met you.

**Benji:** Still scared. Still here.

I read it about fifteen times.

I typed *I'm so glad you're home* and deleted it. I typed *I love you* and deleted that too, because he'd said no big declarations, and I was pretty sure *I love you* at 12:14 a.m. counted as big.

In the end I wrote:

**Me:** Still here too. Tomorrow.

He sent back a thumbs-up. Just a thumbs-up. Somehow that felt like more.

*Slow*. I didn't know what slow looked like. I'd never done anything slow in my life. When I came out, it was like jumping off a cliff. When I fell for Benji, it was like falling off the same cliff again.

Slow meant not knowing. It meant starting over every day, like he did. It meant I couldn't fix this by being strong for him.

I thought about my own speech. *Being brave means that you aren't afraid to be scared*.

It was a lot easier to say into a microphone.
