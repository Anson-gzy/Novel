# Chapter 01: Ground Level

*(POV: Victor)*

The thing nobody tells you about Ferris wheels is that they don't stop when the moment's over.

Benji and I were at the top, and I'd just said the most honest thing I'd ever said to anyone, and he was smiling at me like it was the most obvious thing in the world. Then the car jerked and started going down again. Slowly. Like the wheel wanted us to sit in it a little longer.

Neither of us said anything on the way down. His knee was against mine. I could hear the carnival getting louder the closer we got to the ground, the music and the bells from the ring toss and somebody screaming on the swings. Up top it had felt like we were the only two people in Atlanta. By the time we got to the bottom we were very much not.

The operator swung the bar open. "All right, lovebirds. Everybody out."

"We're not..." I started, and then stopped, because we kind of were. I looked at Benji.

"Thanks, man," Benji said to the operator, and stepped out first. Then he turned around and held his hand out to me.

I took it. It was cold. Mine was probably colder.

"VICTOR SALAZAR."

I knew that voice. Everyone in a two-block radius knew that voice.

Felix was pushing through the line with a giant stuffed banana under one arm. Behind him were Mia and Andrew, and behind them were Lake and Lucy, holding hands and looking like they'd been sprinting.

"We saw," Felix said, out of breath. "We were at the dart game, and Lake said, 'Isn't that Benji getting on the Ferris wheel?' and I said, 'No way, Benji's on a plane to Connecticut,' and she said, 'Felix, I know what Benji looks like,' and then I missed the balloon because I was looking. So I'd like everyone to know that this banana was earned under pressure."

"It's not a banana," Lucy said. "It's a plantain."

"It's a banana with ambition," Felix said. Then he looked at me and Benji, at our hands, and he dropped the whole act. His face just went soft. "Dude."

"Yeah," I said.

"Dude."

"I know."

He hugged me with the banana still in his arm, so I got a face full of yellow fuzz. Over his shoulder I saw Mia with both hands over her mouth.

"I thought you left," she said to Benji.

"I almost did," Benji said.

Mia hugged me next, fast and hard, and then she hugged Benji too, which I don't think he expected. He patted her back like he wasn't sure what the rules were.

"Happy for you, man," Andrew said, and he held his fist out to Benji. Benji bumped it. Andrew nodded at me. "Salazar."

"Spencer."

"That speech today was pretty good, by the way. Bravery and all that."

"Pretty good?" Mia said.

"It was great," Andrew said. "Don't tell him I said great."

Lake had her phone halfway out of her pocket. Lucy put a hand over it without even looking.

"I wasn't going to post it," Lake said.

"You were going to post it," Lucy said.

"I was going to post it tastefully." Lake put the phone away and smiled at me. "Congrats, Victor. Seriously."

Everyone was talking at once and I couldn't keep up with any of it. I just kept feeling Benji's hand in mine, making sure it was still there.

"Okay, I hate to be the sad one again," Mia said. "But I have one week left. One. So all of you are hanging out with me every day until I leave. That's not a request."

"Every day?" Andrew said.

"You especially."

"I mean, obviously me especially."

Lake linked her arm through Mia's. "We'll make a schedule."

"Please don't make a schedule," Lucy said.

"I'm making a schedule."

Felix leaned toward me and lowered his voice. "Hey. Your family's over by the funnel cake stand. Your mom was asking where you went. I didn't say anything."

"Thanks."

"I figured it was your story to tell." He looked at Benji. "Good to have you back, man."

"Thanks, Felix."

Felix wiggled the banana at us like a blessing and followed the others toward the games.

And then it was just the two of us again, standing by the exit of the Ferris wheel with people pushing past us.

"Walk with me?" Benji said. "My mom's in the parking lot."

---

The lot was on the far side of the carnival, past the food trucks, where the lights got thinner and the music turned into a thump you could feel more than hear. Our breath was coming out in clouds. I'd forgotten how cold it was.

"So your mom drove you," I said.

"Yeah."

"Your mom. The same mom who was standing next to your dad at the car two hours ago."

"The same one." Benji put his hands in his pockets. "After you left, we sat in the driveway for a really long time. And then we went inside and sat in the kitchen for a really long time."

"And you told them."

"I told them I'm not going to Loomis Chaffee." He said it plainly, like he was reading it off a list. "I told them I'm staying at Creekwood. I told them I'm going to keep going to meetings and keep calling my sponsor and do all of it, but I'm doing it here."

I stopped walking. "You're staying."

"I'm staying."

I'd been so focused on the wheel, on the *room for one more*, that I hadn't let myself think about what came after. Connecticut had felt like a door slamming. And now it just wasn't there anymore.

"What did your dad say?"

Benji was quiet for a second. "A lot. Not a lot of it was good." He looked at me. "I'm not going to tell you all of it. Some of it's between me and him. But he knows it's my choice. And my mom's the one who said she'd drive me here, so. That's something."

"That's a lot of something."

"Yeah." He almost smiled. "It is."

We started walking again. I could see a car at the end of the row with its headlights off and the engine running. The exhaust was drifting up white in the dark.

"Victor," Benji said. "Before we get there."

"Okay."

"I meant everything I said up there. I want this. I want us. But I need you to hear the other part too, because I don't think you heard it."

"I heard it."

"You heard 'I want us to be together.' I need you to hear 'I still have to work on myself.'" He stopped at the edge of the lot and turned so he was facing me. "I can't do big things right now. No big declarations. No grand plans. I have to take this one day at a time, because that's how I stay sober. And I can't make you the thing that holds me together. If I do that, and something goes wrong between us, I don't know what happens to me. Does that make sense?"

It did, and it didn't. My whole body wanted to say *I'll hold you together, that's what I'm here for*. That's kind of what I do. It's what I've always done with my family. Victor's the strong one. Victor's the rock.

But I thought about what his dad had said to me. *You're the reason he relapsed*. And I thought maybe this was Benji's way of saying: *you're not, and you don't get to be the reason I don't, either*.

"Slow," I said.

"Slow."

"I can do slow."

He raised an eyebrow. "Can you?"

"No," I said. "Probably not. But I can try."

That got a real laugh out of him, the kind where his shoulders move. "That's honest, at least."

"I'm working on honest."

"Me too."

The car's headlights flicked on. Then off again. Then on.

"That's my mom's way of saying hurry up," Benji said.

"Should I say hi?"

"You don't have to."

"I know. Should I?"

He thought about it. "Yeah. I think you should."

We walked to the car. The window rolled down, and Benji's mom looked out at me. She had the same eyes as him. She looked tired. She looked like someone who'd been crying earlier and had fixed her face in the rearview mirror.

"Hi, Mrs. Campbell," I said.

"Victor." She nodded once. It wasn't cold, exactly. It was careful. Like she was holding something heavy and didn't want to drop it in front of me. "That was a very nice speech today. Benji told me about it."

"Oh. Thanks."

"It's late," she said. "He's had a very long day."

"Right. Of course."

Benji squeezed my hand one more time and let go. He went around to the passenger side, and before he got in, he looked at me over the roof of the car.

"Tomorrow?" he said.

"Tomorrow."

The door shut. The car pulled out, and I watched the taillights until they turned onto the road, and then I stood there in the parking lot by myself for a minute, because I needed to.

*I'm staying*.

I said it once in my head just to hear it again.

---

My family was right where Felix said they'd be, at a picnic table by the funnel cake stand. Adrian had powdered sugar on his face all the way to his eyebrows. Pilar was on her phone. Mom and Dad were sitting on the same side of the table, shoulder to shoulder, looking at something on Mom's phone and laughing.

Mom saw me first. "Mijo! There you are. Where did you run off to?"

"We were about to send out a search party," Dad said. "Adrian was going to lead it."

"I was going to get another funnel cake first," Adrian said.

I sat down across from them. I didn't know how to start, so I just started.

"Benji's staying," I said. "He's not going to Connecticut. He came to the carnival. We, um. We rode the Ferris wheel."

Nobody said anything for a second. Pilar looked up from her phone.

Then Mom said, "Ay, Victor," and got up and came around the table and hugged me from behind, her chin on top of my head.

"Hold on," Dad said. "This afternoon you said it didn't work out."

"It didn't," I said. "And then it did."

"So the dramatic sprint worked," Pilar said. "Eventually."

Mom sat down next to me instead of going back to her side. She was smiling, but I could see something else in her face too. She knew what this year had been like for Benji. She knew what it had been like for me.

"And how is he?" she asked. "Is he okay?"

"He's working on it," I said. "He said we have to go slow. That he has to put his recovery first."

"Good," Dad said, and he meant it. "That's a smart kid."

"Slow is good," Mom said. "Slow is very good." She tucked my hair behind my ear like I was eight. "I'm proud of you, mi amor. Two times in one day."

"Okay, you guys are gonna make me cry at a carnival."

"You already made your dad sob," Pilar said. "It's only fair."

Dad pointed a funnel cake at her. "I had something in my eye."

"For the whole speech?"

"It was a big thing."

---

At home, Adrian fell asleep on the couch in his coat, and Dad carried him to bed like a sack of laundry. Mom went to take her makeup off. I was in my room trying to figure out where to put a Bravery Award, which is harder than it sounds, because anywhere you put it, it looks like you're showing off.

Pilar knocked on the open door and leaned against the frame.

"So," she said. "Benji."

"Benji."

"Are you happy?"

I thought about it. "Yeah. And scared. Mostly both."

"That sounds like you." She came in and sat on the end of my bed and looked at the award in my hands. "Put it on the bookshelf. Behind something. Then it's there but it's not, like, *there*."

I did. It was a good idea. I told her so, and she rolled her eyes.

"Can I ask you something?" I said.

"You're going to anyway."

"You and Felix. Earlier. I saw you pull him into the family picture."

Pilar picked at a thread on my comforter. "He apologized. Like, a real apology. Not a Felix-speech apology. A real one." She shrugged. "We're not back together. We're just... not broken anymore. He's family. He was family before he was my boyfriend. I don't want to lose that part."

"That's really mature."

"Don't make it weird."

"I'm not making it weird. I'm just saying you're growing up."

"And you're turning into Mom." She stood up. "Night, Victor."

"Night."

She stopped at the door. "Hey. For what it's worth. I'm glad he didn't leave."

"Me too."

---

I didn't sleep. I lay in bed with the lights off and my phone on my chest, and I kept replaying the wheel. His knee against mine. *That isn't our story. This is*.

At 12:14, my phone buzzed.

**Benji:** Home. Called my sponsor. Told them everything. They say hi, by the way, which is weird because they've never met you.

**Benji:** Still scared. Still here.

I read it about fifteen times.

I typed *I'm so glad you're home* and deleted it. I typed *I love you* and deleted that too, because he'd said no big declarations, and I was pretty sure *I love you* at 12:14 a.m. counted as big.

In the end I wrote:

**Me:** Still here too. Tomorrow.

He sent back a thumbs-up. Just a thumbs-up. And somehow that felt like more.

I put the phone face down on my chest and stared at the ceiling. *Slow*. I didn't know what slow looked like. I'd never done anything slow in my life. When I came out, it was like jumping off a cliff. When I fell for Benji, it was like falling off the same cliff again.

Slow meant not knowing. It meant every day, starting over, like he did. It meant I couldn't fix it by being strong for him.

I thought about my own speech. *Being brave means that you aren't afraid to be scared*.

It was a lot easier to say into a microphone.
