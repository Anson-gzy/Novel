# Chapter 25: Midnight Diner

*(Third-Person Limited — POV: Zack)*

At three in the morning on Saturday, the sound of the Pacific Ocean was shut out behind the heavy glass of the car windows.

Zack parked his mother's Audi Q5 beneath the shadow of a eucalyptus tree along the curve of Castellammare. The dashboard emitted a faint blue glow. A single text from Wade sat on the screen.

*wade: get your knee checked by a real doctor. and whoever you are, the dowel tip was actually solid.*

Zack stared at the text for a few seconds. He typed *“Thanks”*, sent it, and deleted the `justin.k` profile entirely. He closed the app, wiped the cache, and tossed his phone onto the passenger seat.

Posing as Justin had felt like an easy shield at first. He had convinced himself that talking to a stranger under a fake name gave him space from the mess with Andrew. But sitting in that dark room in Pacific Palisades and seeing Andrew’s name pop up in Wade’s follower list had killed the illusion instantly. Dressing up in someone else’s life did not fix his own.

He drove home along the quiet coastal highway.

The morning fog was thick when he pulled into his driveway around four. The streetlights bled pale yellow circles into the mist. As soon as he killed the engine, he saw someone standing by the side porch.

Andrew was wearing his black heavy-cotton hoodie, the hood pulled low over his forehead. He had his hands jammed into his front pocket. Condensation coated the shoulders of his sweatshirt. He had been standing there in the cold for a long time.

Zack sat in the car for thirty seconds. His chest felt heavy. His fingers tightened around the leather steering wheel.

For weeks, Zack had viewed Andrew as someone who moved through life without friction. Andrew could walk down a hallway full of rumors without turning his head. He could switch between charm and complete indifference without breaking a sweat. When Andrew started bringing up Justin in conversation, Zack had assumed it was part of that same pattern: Andrew was just someone who needed an audience, someone who moved on whenever a new face appeared.

Zack pushed the car door open and stepped out.

At the sound of the door closing, Andrew turned. His face was pale. Dark circles sat under his eyes, and his mouth was set in a tight, rigid line. That was the face he made whenever he was losing control of a situation.

"Where were you?" Andrew asked. His voice was raspy from the cold air.

"Around," Zack said. He walked up the stone steps, keeping his voice flat. "You shouldn't be standing out here. It's freezing. Does Ethan know where you are?"

Andrew did not answer the question. He pulled his phone from his pocket. The screen lit up with dozens of unread texts and missed calls, all sent to Zack's number over the last twelve hours.

"You stopped answering," Andrew said, looking straight at him. "I thought something happened to your car. Or that you decided to stop talking to me."

"I was tired, Andrew," Zack said, reaching for the door handle. "If you came to check if I was still breathing, I am. You can go home."

Andrew took a step closer. His sneakers squeaked against the wet stone. He smelled like damp air, faint tobacco, and cedarwood.

"I went to your pool," Andrew said.

Zack paused, his hand still on the brass handle. "What?"

"You missed practice on Friday. Your physical therapist said your shoulder needed work, but you weren't at the facility." Andrew reached into his hoodie pocket and pulled out a small black leather notebook. He held it out. "And it dropped below fifty degrees tonight. Back in July, the doctor told you your left knee gets stiff when the humidity jumps."

Zack looked at the notebook.

It was not an app or a digital note. It was a physical pocket notebook with worn edges. Andrew flipped it open with one thumb. The pages were covered in neat, heavy ink handwriting:

*June 18: Left knee rehab. Keep leg warm during damp weather.*
*July 04: Dislikes overly sweet pancakes. Black coffee with two raw sugar cubes.*
*Sept 12: Holds his left wrist whenever Justin's name comes up.*
*Oct 05: Said 'go ahead' today. His eyes didn't move. He was lying.*

Zack stared at the lines.

The ink was pressed deep into the paper. The entries ran across months, starting from the hospital after the gala and continuing through Andrew's transfer to West Valley High.

"You thought I was just careless, didn't you?" Andrew's voice was quiet, laced with a bitter edge. "You thought I moved to public school, met new people, and started talking about Justin because I wanted someone easier. Someone who didn't come with history."

Zack said nothing. His throat felt dry.

"I was terrified," Andrew said. The composure in his face cracked, revealing something raw underneath. "In that basement, when everything collapsed, you were the only person who held my hand. When we got out, I had no idea how to keep that alive. I was scared that if I stayed too close, I would smother you. I tried to act like a normal person. I tried talking to Justin to prove to myself that I wasn't completely broken. But it was all fake."

Andrew took a ragged breath. His hand holding the notebook trembled slightly.

"I can't go a day without checking if you called," he said. "The notebook... I kept it because I was afraid that if my head got too loud, I'd lose track of the things that actually mattered about you."

The morning wind blew through the trees above the driveway.

Zack stood on the porch, looking at the boy in front of him. The arrogant, untouchable posture was completely gone. Andrew looked small in the damp air, holding out a notebook like a shield, waiting for a verdict.

Zack had spent weeks convincing himself that walking away was the smart move. But looking at Andrew now, he realized he had misread the whole thing. Andrew's distance had never been about carelessness. It was the desperate, clumsy panic of someone who had lost his family and had no idea how to hold onto the one person he had left.

"Andrew," Zack said softly.

"Come with me," Andrew said. He grabbed Zack's wrist. His fingers were ice cold, but his grip was firm. "I know a place. We're going now."

Andrew did not take his BMW. He flagged down a yellow cab at the corner of the avenue.

They rode in silence for thirty minutes as the cab navigated the empty commercial streets. The car pulled up to a brick building on the corner of an old intersection.

It was a classic twenty-four-hour American diner.

A faded yellow neon sign hummed softly in the mist, displaying *24 HOURS OPEN*. A small brass bell chimed above the door as Andrew pushed it open.

The diner was completely empty.

The air inside smelled of brewed coffee, heated maple syrup, and old vinyl booths. A jukebox in the back corner played a slow, muted jazz track. An elderly waiter behind the counter gave them a brief nod and went back to wiping glass mugs.

They sat in a corner booth near the front window.

The high red vinyl seats enclosed them in a small, private space. Outside, the street was dark and still. Inside, a low-hanging pendant light cast a warm yellow glow across the Formica table.

The waiter brought two plates of pancakes topped with melting butter, along with a pot of black coffee and two raw sugar cubes.

"You remember this place?" Andrew asked, sliding the coffee cup toward Zack.

"June," Zack said, his voice quiet. "The week after the hospital. You took me to a place like this."

"That was the first one," Andrew said, leaning his forearms on the table. "You couldn't eat solid food back then because of your ribs. You just drank half a glass of milk. I spent the whole night hoping that one day we'd sit here normally."

Andrew set his fork down. He crossed his hands on the table and looked directly at Zack.

"I'm sorry, Zack."

The words were plain and unpolished.

"I shouldn't have brought Justin into our space. I shouldn't have put on that silver bracelet and then pulled back when things got real. I told myself I was giving you room, but I was just being a coward. I was afraid to admit how much I needed you."

Andrew leaned forward slightly.

"That bracelet... I put it on the day you gave it to me, and I've never taken it off. No matter what I told anyone else, the only person who ever mattered was you."

Zack sat across from him, looking down at his plate.

The butter was melting slowly, running down the side of the pancakes into the syrup.

All the frustration he had buried for months—the quiet bitterness of watching Andrew from a distance, the self-doubt that had driven him to hide behind a fake online profile—seemed to collapse all at once under the weight of Andrew's words.

Zack tried to keep his face steady. He reached for his coffee, but his fingers shook.

A single tear dropped onto the paper napkin beside his plate.

Then another followed.

Zack turned his head toward the window, trying to wipe his eyes with his sleeve. He did not sob out loud, but his shoulders shook with quiet, heavy breaths.

Andrew did not hesitate. He slid out of his side of the booth and sat down next to Zack. He pulled Zack into a tight embrace.

Zack rested his forehead against Andrew's shoulder. The cold fabric of the hoodie pressed against his cheek, carrying the scent of rain and coffee. Zack gripped the back of Andrew's sweatshirt, burying his face in his shoulder.

"You're an idiot," Zack muttered, his voice muffled by the cloth.

"I know," Andrew whispered, his hand resting on the back of Zack's neck. "I know. You can be as mad as you want. Just don't walk away."

The jazz music played softly in the background. The waiter stayed behind the counter, leaving them alone.

Zack held onto Andrew until his breathing slowed down. When he finally pulled back, his eyes were red, but the weight in his chest was gone.

Andrew pulled a paper napkin from the dispenser and gently wiped the moisture from Zack's face.

"The pancakes are getting cold," Andrew said quietly.

"I don't care," Zack said. He picked up his fork and took a bite.

The sweet syrup tasted rich and warm.

Andrew watched him, a genuine smile breaking across his face. It was not the polished, magnetic smile he used at school. It was small, quiet, and completely real.

"After this," Andrew said, reaching out to cover Zack's hand, "we're not hiding anything."

Zack squeezed his fingers back.

"Good."

Outside the diner window, the first gray light of dawn began to push back the dark.
