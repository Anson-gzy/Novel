### Chapter 2: "Group Work"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,800
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Justin checks schedule — one shared class. Relief.
  2. Group work assigned. Justin pushes himself to approach Andrew and pair up.

- **Foreshadowing:**

  - Justin's impulse to find "an opening" in Andrew — foreshadows deeper investment
  - The "I trust you" moment — first deviation from Andrew's cold pattern
  - Andrew's research notes with "use whatever structure you want" — giving autonomy, not control
- **Hook:** Andrew's unexpected "I trust you" — transforms Justin's vague unease into active curiosity.

### Chapter 1: "The Transfer Kid"
**Summary:** Justin's Instagram group chat explodes with news that a private school student is transferring to West Valley High after losing both parents. Reactions range from sympathy to hostility about the "private school" label. Justin stays indifferent. On the first day, Andrew walks the hallway with a self-possession that reads as arrogance — he doesn't react to the stares, the whispers, or the mockery from the football guys. Justin watches and finds himself unsettled but can't articulate why. He's then called to Principal Nakamura's office and asked to "look after" Andrew — show him around, check in. Justin is annoyed but agrees. At lunch, he vents to Dylan, Samira, and Tyler about the absurdity of being assigned to babysit an all-AP student when he barely manages C-pluses. His friends joke, but Justin leaves the conversation with a nagging sense that Andrew is something he can't easily file away.

### Chapter 2: "Group Work"
**Summary:** Justin checks his schedule and discovers he shares only one class with Andrew — World History. He feels relieved. When Mr. Reeves assigns group work, Justin forces himself to approach Andrew and suggest they pair up, driven by his promise to Nakamura. The introduction is painfully flat: Andrew says "sure" and nothing more. During the work period, Andrew's intellectual speed is immediately apparent — he processes information effortlessly while Justin struggles to keep up, reduced to making a title slide. Justin keeps searching for any emotional crack in Andrew's surface and finds nothing. Then, at the bell, Justin suggests meeting after school to finish. Andrew refuses and tells Justin to handle the slides himself. Justin protests — he's a C-plus student who contributed nothing. Andrew looks at him, really looks for the first time, and says "I trust you." He leaves without another word. Justin is left confused: after forty-five minutes of complete blankness, three unexpected words crack the wall open an inch. He finds Andrew's organized research notes in his inbox with a note saying "use whatever structure you want." Dylan notices Justin is describing the whole class period in unusual detail. Justin realizes he can't file Andrew away as just "weird kid" anymore — he's actively curious, and he doesn't know what to do with that.

### Chapter 3: "Mutual Followers"
**Summary:** Justin hangs out with his friend group after school — comfortable, easy, but he's quieter than usual. Something sits in the back of his mind. That evening, he searches Andrew's Instagram and follows him. Andrew's profile is sparse: fifty-two followers, no bio, eight posts over a year that reveal nothing — except an eight-month gap and a single recent photo of an empty hallway. Over the next few days, Justin starts unconsciously tracking Andrew's patterns: the hallway he comes from, his arrival time, where he goes after school. He watches the cafeteria for him. He positions himself at his locker at 3:15. He realizes he's paying a kind of attention he didn't agree to. A random thought strikes: where does Andrew go home to? He asks Samira sideways and learns Andrew lives with an older brother, twenty-two or twenty-three, who dropped out of college to take custody. The information lands heavier than Justin expects. When his friends bring up Andrew again at lunch — teasing about the babysitting assignment — Justin shuts it down and changes the subject. Walking to class, he recognizes that the things he knows about Andrew feel like they belong somewhere private, away from the jokes.

### Chapter 4: "Tiffany"
**Summary:** Thursday. Andrew walks into World History and says "hey" to Justin — unprompted, voluntary, like it's something they do. Justin is stunned by a single syllable. He notices a silver bracelet on Andrew's wrist — Tiffany, thin, catching the light. It doesn't fit the cold image. Justin compliments it, and Andrew actually responds: someone gave it to him, someone who isn't around anymore. He doesn't elaborate. During work on the Cold War slides, Andrew leans over and asks about Justin's progress. Justin admits it's a mess. Andrew doesn't judge — he offers a clear suggestion and says "it'll be fine," echoing the quiet trust from the week before. They talk through the rest of class — nothing deep, but real and easy. Justin keeps waiting for Andrew to close back up. He doesn't. At the bell, Andrew asks if Justin wants to come over after school. His place. Justin says yes before he has time to think about it. He walks out with sweating hands.

### Chapter 5: "Probably a Party"
**Summary:** At lunch, Justin's friends are already armed with intelligence: Andrew has been friendlier this week, and multiple people noticed. Dylan connects the dots — Andrew's behavioral shift started when Justin entered his orbit. The teasing starts. Dylan says Andrew "clearly likes" Justin. Justin deflates internally. He'd been privately, secretly holding a belief that Andrew's attention was specific — aimed at him. Hearing his friends talk about Andrew's general friendliness shrinks that belief. What if Justin is just one of many? What if the invitation is a group thing? He spirals. At home, he changes his shirt. Then changes it back. Then changes it again. He's disgusted with himself for caring. Then he realizes: he doesn't have Andrew's number, address, or any way to get to his place. Andrew hadn't given him any details. Justin opens Instagram to message him but can't figure out what to say. He types and deletes three messages. The chapter ends with Justin sitting on his bed, dressed, phone dark, not moving — wanting something he can't articulate and afraid of what happens if he goes.

### Chapter 6: "Penthouse"
**Summary:** Andrew resolves Justin's paralysis by calling — not texting, calling — on Justin's actual phone number, which he shouldn't have. Justin almost doesn't pick up. Andrew asks if he still wants to come over and offers to drive. Justin asks how Andrew got his number; Andrew says "I asked around" and moves on. Justin lets it go. Andrew pulls up in a BMW — not the hand-me-down sedan Justin imagined. The drive is short and easy; Andrew talks, asks questions, doesn't perform. Justin relaxes without deciding to. They arrive at a penthouse — fourteenth floor, floor-to-ceiling windows, real furniture, a marble kitchen. Every assumption Justin had about Andrew's financial situation collapses instantly. Andrew's older brother Ethan is home — twenty-three or twenty-four, calm, working on a laptop with two phones and a legal pad. He's clearly running something, not holding things together. Justin recalibrates everything: the public school transfer, the reinvention, the AP courses — none of it was about money. Something else pushed Andrew out. They hang out in Andrew's room — watch a show, talk about nothing. Andrew shows Justin his headphones and a playlist, which Justin recognizes as the first deliberate act of sharing Andrew has offered (the bracelet and "I trust you" were accidental leaks; this was chosen). Justin takes the Metro home. He watches the city shift from Andrew's version to his own and arrives in a quietly devastated state — unable to sleep, feeling that his world has gotten smaller and the trouble he can sense but can't name is pressing in from every side.

### Chapter 7: "Marcus Talks First" *(Marcus POV)*
**Summary:** First non-Justin chapter. Marcus sits down at lunch — Justin not yet present — and reveals plainly that Andrew approached him specifically to ask for Justin's phone number and address, and requested that Marcus keep it quiet. Dylan builds an immediate theory; Samira goes silent and gets a look that means she's already several steps ahead; Becca asks careful questions and calls it "kind of sweet," which Samira counters with "or it's controlling." When Justin arrives, the topic switches instantly. He mentions, almost as a side note, that he still doesn't know how Andrew got his number. The table goes quiet for half a second. Nobody cracks. Justin looks around, reads the silence, and drops it — not because he believes them, but because he's not ready for the answer. Marcus reflects: he's not covering for Andrew out of loyalty; he said he wouldn't tell, so he won't. He doesn't know what to think of Andrew yet. But he notices Justin is off — saying "fine" three times and meaning none of them. Whatever is happening between Justin and Andrew, Marcus can see it's not nothing.

### Chapter 8: "Schedule Change" *(Marcus POV continued)*
**Summary:** Marcus's world is established: AP English, standard History, AP Government, a preference for saying what he thinks without decoration. Andrew shows up in Marcus's fourth-period Government class partway through the semester — a schedule correction from AP Econ he'd already taken. Marcus recognizes him and they exchange a nod of mutual acknowledgment. Over the following weeks, Marcus observes a version of Andrew that Justin never sees: quieter, operating at seventy percent, not performing the easy confidence. Andrew is occasionally strange — trailing off mid-sentence during class discussions, making comments about "oversight versus trust" and "the right to reinvent yourself" that clearly refer to something personal. Marcus finds this mildly annoying; he prefers people who finish their sentences. He mentions to Dylan that Andrew is "weird," and Dylan finds that funny. The chapter ends with Marcus seeing Andrew in the hallway talking to Mia from AP Bio — the full magnetism restored, charm at maximum, the crowd subtly bending around him. Marcus watches and confronts the fact that Andrew is not one thing: he's at least two people, and which one you get depends on who's watching. He decides it's not his problem. Then he keeps watching anyway.

### Chapter 9: "Invisible" *(Marcus POV continued)*
**Summary:** Marcus establishes a pattern: Andrew knows exactly who he is — they share Government, they've talked, they've exchanged nods — but in hallways and unstructured spaces, Andrew looks straight through him like he's part of the architecture. Not hostile. Just absent. Marcus tests it twice: once at Andrew's locker (half-second of eye contact, then nothing), once in a class discussion (functional engagement, then the wall drops). He brings it up with Dylan (who jokes), with Samira (who says "maybe he's just like that with some people" and then reads Marcus: "You're bringing it up in the library on a Tuesday. It bothers you."). At lunch, Marcus asks the full table if anyone else has noticed. Justin immediately and instinctively defends Andrew — "His parents died, Marcus" — with a speed that reveals more about Justin's investment than Justin realizes. The group teases Justin for the defensiveness. Marcus files away the reaction: Justin went to bat for someone he claimed to barely know, automatically, like defending someone who matters.

### Chapter 10: "Deterioration" *(Marcus POV continued)*
**Summary:** Time passes. Marcus notices a new silence: Justin stops mentioning Andrew. Not dramatically — just a subtraction. The group doesn't push it. Marcus watches from both sides. Justin is fine on the surface but operating at "ninety-five percent speed" — the timing is off in ways only someone who knows him well can detect. In Government, Andrew has dropped from seventy to sixty percent — the trailed-off sentences have stopped entirely. Marcus's private read: Andrew showed his real self and the warmth was temporary. He feels vindicated and sorry for Justin simultaneously. The chapter's buried secret: Marcus has started to like Andrew. Not as a label or a crush — just an interest that grew past clinical observation into something with a pulse. He can't explain it logically, keeps it entirely to himself, and recognizes it as bad judgment that he's committing anyway. The friend group goes quiet around Andrew's name. Samira notices. Nobody says anything.

### Chapter 11: "Not You, Not Him" *(Marcus POV continued)*
**Summary:** Marcus runs out of patience in early December. He replays the contrast — Andrew's warmth toward Justin versus total blankness toward him — and starts wondering if Andrew's interest in Justin was ever more than friendly. He asks around quietly (Dylan, Becca) without convincing himself it's general curiosity. In Government class, during independent work time, Marcus confronts Andrew directly: "What's your problem with me?" Andrew's response is plain, factual, delivered without cruelty: stop. He's not interested. He's not interested in Justin either. He has a partner — someone from his old school. "Justin is a different situation" and he won't discuss it further. The conversation is short. Marcus absorbs it, turns back to his notebook, and nobody in the room knows anything happened. That night, he sits with the deflated feeling alone. He doesn't tell Dylan, Samira, Becca, Tyler, or Justin. He tells himself he's fine. He lies in the dark and waits for the feeling to pass.

### Chapter 12: "First Punch" *(Marcus POV — cliffhanger)*
**Summary:** Thursday lunch, second week of December. The table is at its usual low hum. Dylan is talking, Becca has her phone out, Tyler is eating chips. Marcus is quieter than normal. Justin is present but not participating — reduced, like someone turned his volume down. Andrew is not mentioned. Samira says something small to Marcus; he half-answers, thinking about the shape of the last few weeks. Then his name — shouted across the cafeteria, cutting through the noise so sharply the whole table looks up at the same time. Justin. He's standing ten feet away with an expression Marcus has never seen — raw, unprocessed, something that bypassed all of Justin's usual filters. Marcus turns. He gets about halfway through the motion before the punch lands on the side of his jaw. He goes down. The tray goes. The cafeteria goes half-silent in concentric circles. Marcus looks up from the floor. Justin is standing over him, hand still half-closed, chest moving with deep deliberate breaths, eyes wet but not crying. Neither of them moves. The cafeteria holds its breath.

### Chapter 13: "The Gala" *(Zack POV — flashback begins)*
**Summary:** Zack Chen introduces himself — a student from Westbrook Prep who knew Andrew Hayes by face and reputation but never by conversation. Andrew was the quiet one, invisible to the school's social machinery. The chapter uses 插叙 (intercut) structure: it opens on the crisis — gunshots, an explosion — and reaches backward into the "before" through fragments triggered by the chaos. Zack remembers the car ride with his parents: his mother's precision (polished shoes left outside his door as a wordless instruction), his father's quarter-second nod across a crowded room (the Chen family check-in). He remembers arriving at the venue, the chandeliers his mother called "original," the tradition of leaving "when they start repeating themselves." He remembers noticing Andrew across the ballroom with two adults — Andrew's father's private half-smile directed at his wife, his mother's real laugh (not the public one), her hand touching Andrew's arm with the casual contact of permanence. "They looked permanent." Then the crisis returns: Zack loses his parents in the surge. He finds Andrew pinned under a collapsed structural column and pulls him free. They're trapped — exits blocked, Zack's leg badly injured. Andrew calls Ethan. The brother's voice is controlled in a way Zack recognizes: someone managing terrible information. Ethan tells Andrew their parents are fine. Andrew exhales. Zack says nothing. The chapter ends with Zack in the dark, holding the weight of what he suspects while thinking about his own parents — the mother who issues instructions as observations, the father who says love through silence.

### Chapter 14: "One Day, One Night" *(Zack POV continued)*
**Summary:** Zack and Andrew are trapped for a full day and night. Zack's injury prevents movement, so they stay still and talk. In the dark, with nothing else to do, honesty becomes lower-risk than usual. Andrew talks about Westbrook — the specific texture of being invisible, of walking into rooms that didn't register his presence. He says it without self-pity, almost like reporting on someone else. Zack talks too — about swim team, parental expectations, the plan for his life that he didn't choose. They reach topics strangers don't normally reach: what they're afraid of, what they'd change, what they thought life would look like. Zack quietly realizes he doesn't want Andrew to disappear once they get out. He doesn't say it. They're found after eighteen hours. The rescue is loud and fast — medical teams, stretchers, immediate separation. Before they're pulled fully apart, Andrew reaches out and grabs Zack's hand. Then he's gone. In the hospital, waiting for surgery on his leg, Zack overhears medical staff discussing casualties: Andrew's parents were pronounced at the scene. Zack goes under anesthesia knowing that Andrew doesn't know yet.

### Chapter 15: "Tiffany, Part Two" *(Zack POV continued)*
**Summary:** Weeks pass. Zack recovers in the hospital, then at home. He and Andrew begin texting — first short check-ins, then longer conversations, then nightly phone calls. Andrew talks about moving in with Ethan, about the legal logistics, about his decision to leave Westbrook and start over at a public school. Zack visits Andrew at the penthouse. The day is easy. They listen to music, eat lunch, watch a show. Zack gives Andrew a Tiffany bracelet — the very bracelet Justin noticed in Chapter 4. He'd bought it on impulse. Andrew puts it on immediately, without hesitation. Zack almost confesses his feelings — has the sentence ready — but lets the moment pass, telling himself there's time. Andrew starts at West Valley High. The first weeks are fine, but gradually Andrew starts mentioning someone: Justin. A guy the principal assigned. At first Zack doesn't register it. Then the name keeps appearing.

### Chapter 16: "Go Find Him" *(Zack POV — flashback ends)*
**Summary:** The Justin mentions accumulate. Zack starts hearing something in them — the specificity, the attention to detail, the way Andrew describes small moments with a frequency that has its own rhythm. Zack tries indirect approaches — a comment, a question — but Andrew either doesn't pick it up or pretends not to. The conversation goes awkward, recovers, and the pattern repeats. Zack recognizes what's happening: Andrew has feelings for Justin that he can't name and won't act on because of his loyalty to Zack. Eventually Zack decides: if Andrew needs permission, he'll give it. On a Saturday phone call, he tells Andrew plainly that if there's someone he wants to spend time with, he should. "As a friend." Andrew says okay. Then he texts Justin. Zack sits with what he just did — doesn't regret it exactly, just registers it. The chapter closes with Zack alone in his room, the silence having the room.

### Chapter 17: "Three Dots" *(Justin POV returns)*
**Summary:** Back in the present timeline. Justin notices something different about Andrew — a gap where the warmth used to be. Andrew still responds, still shows up, still makes eye contact, but there's a distance. Justin notices Andrew's phone is face-up more often, the typing indicator appearing and disappearing. He doesn't ask who. Through the friend group, the Marcus situation reaches him — Marcus has been weird, asking about Andrew, paying unusual attention. Samira knows something she won't share. Justin starts running math in a direction he doesn't like: Marcus and Andrew, something between them. He doesn't confront anyone. He watches, waits, and gets increasingly bad at sleeping. ### Chapter 18: "The Gift" *(Justin POV continued)*
**Summary:** Justin decides to do something. He buys Andrew a specific book Andrew mentioned once, weeks ago — a gesture that reveals how closely he's been listening. They go out to a Thai restaurant; the evening is genuinely good, present, like the early days. Back at the apartment, Ethan isn't home. ### Chapter 19: "Marcus" *(Justin POV — leads into Ch. 12)*
**Summary:** Justin walks, takes the train, sits at home. The sequence replays: the bracelet, the phone, Marcus going weird, Andrew never explaining. He starts placing things — Marcus asking about Andrew too much, the group going quiet, Samira's non-answers. Returns to the table. Sees Marcus sitting there, steady and contained, and something clicks — the wrong click, built on wrong math. He walks over, says Marcus's name. Marcus starts to turn. ### Chapter 25: "Midnight Diner" *(Third-Person Limited POV)*

### Chapter 28: "Ice Pack" *(Justin POV)*
**Summary:** Following his confrontation with Andrew outside the East Gate, Justin takes the Metro home in a daze with a three-day suspension and throbbing, swollen knuckles.
