## Detailed Chapter Plans

### Chapter 1: "The Transfer Kid"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,800
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Instagram group chat blowing up — private school kid transferring in. Mixed reactions. Justin says he doesn't care.
  2. First day. Andrew walks the hallway like he owns it. Crowd forms. Justin watches from a distance — finds him arrogant. Negative first impressions.
  3. Justin is called to principal's office and asked to look after Andrew. Annoyed. Agrees anyway.
  4. Justin vents to friends. Notes absurdity of babysitting an AP student.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Justin moves from indifference → mild curiosity (against his will) → annoyance at being assigned → resigned acceptance
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Andrew's unreadable affect — is it armor or emptiness?
  - Justin's inability to name what unsettles him about Andrew
  - The gap between public perception (arrogant) and reality (unknown)
- **Hook:** Justin agrees to something he doesn't understand, and the reader senses he's already noticed Andrew more than he admits

### Chapter 2: "Group Work"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,800
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Justin checks schedule — one shared class. Relief.
  2. Group work assigned. Justin pushes himself to approach Andrew and pair up.
  3. Introduction. Andrew: minimal words, not hostile, not interested. Awkward silence.
  4. They work. Andrew is clearly sharp — leads without trying. Justin follows without meaning to.
  5. After class, Justin says they should meet up. Andrew refuses — tells Justin to do the slides himself. Justin protests his bad grades. Andrew looks at him (really looks, for the first time) and says "I trust you." Leaves. Justin is confused and curious.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Relief → reluctant obligation → discomfort at the silence → grudging respect for Andrew's competence → confusion at "I trust you" → active curiosity
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Andrew's efficiency and emotional blankness as two sides of the same coin
  - Justin's impulse to find "an opening" in Andrew — foreshadows deeper investment
  - The "I trust you" moment — first deviation from Andrew's cold pattern
  - Andrew's research notes with "use whatever structure you want" — giving autonomy, not control
- **Hook:** Andrew's unexpected "I trust you" — transforms Justin's vague unease into active curiosity.

### Chapter 3: "Mutual Followers"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,800
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Justin hangs with friend group — comfortable, loose. Gossip about Justin himself. Andrew's name doesn't come up yet but Justin is quieter than usual.
  2. Alone in his room, Justin searches Andrew's Instagram, follows him. Scrolls through posts — the feed doesn't give much away.
  3. Over the next couple of days, Justin starts low-key tracking Andrew's patterns — hallway routes, class timing, after-school direction. Not on purpose. Or so he tells himself.
  4. It hits him: where does Andrew live? Who does he go home to? He finds out Andrew lives with an older brother. This lands heavier than expected.
  5. Friends bring up Andrew again — teasing about the principal assignment. Justin shuts it down fast.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Comfort → restless curiosity → covert attention → empathy hitting unexpectedly → defensive denial
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Instagram follow as first conscious act of reaching toward Andrew
  - Pattern-tracking behavior — investment disguised as indifference
  - Andrew's older brother — new backstory element
  - Justin shutting down friend teasing — protectiveness he hasn't earned or acknowledged
- **Hook:** Justin knows more about Andrew than he should, and he's starting to notice that he notices.

### Chapter 4: "Tiffany"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,800
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Thursday. Andrew greets Justin when he walks into class — casual, easy. This is new.
  2. Justin notices a silver Tiffany bracelet on Andrew's wrist. Doesn't fit the cold image.
  3. Justin compliments the bracelet. Andrew actually talks — says someone gave it to him. Doesn't say who.
  4. Working on the group slides. Andrew asks how Justin's section is going. Justin is honest — it's a mess. Andrew says something encouraging.
  5. They talk through the rest of class — small things, nothing deep, but real. Justin keeps waiting for Andrew to go cold. He doesn't.
  6. End of class: Andrew asks if Justin wants to come over after school. Justin says yes before he thinks about it.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Surprise at the greeting → curiosity about the bracelet → genuine exchange → comfort in the conversation → impulsive yes → delayed realization of what he agreed to
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - The Tiffany bracelet — gift from whom? (plants mystery for later)
  - Andrew initiating contact — the wall is opening from both sides now
  - The invitation — first time Andrew actively seeks Justin's company outside school
- **Hook:** Justin said yes to going to Andrew's place, and he doesn't know what that means yet.

### Chapter 5: "Probably a Party"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,800
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Lunch. Friends heard something — teasing starts. Someone jokes Andrew clearly likes Justin. Another says Andrew's mood has shifted this week.
  2. Justin laughs it off but internally the feeling shrinks. What if Andrew is like this with everyone? What if the invite means nothing?
  3. Spiral continues. What if it's a party? What if Justin is just one of many people invited? Gets quieter. Friends notice but don't push.
  4. Walks home. Gets ready — changes outfit once or twice. Annoyed at himself for caring.
  5. Sits and waits. Doubt comes back louder. Andrew hasn't texted. Justin doesn't know how to start. Chapter ends on paralysis — dressed, ready, not moving.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Surface confidence → private deflation → spiraling self-doubt → caring more than he wants to → paralysis
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Justin's investment is no longer deniable — he changed his outfit
  - The gap between public composure and private anxiety mirrors Andrew's own mask
  - Friends as external barometer — they see something Justin won't name
- **Hook:** Justin is frozen, wanting something he can't articulate and afraid of what happens if he goes.

### Chapter 6: "Penthouse"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~2,000
- **POV:** Justin (1st person)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Andrew calls Justin — not texts, calls. Justin is caught off guard. He almost doesn't pick up. Andrew asks if he still wants to come over, says he can pick him up. Justin asks how Andrew has his number. Andrew gives a vague non-answer. Justin lets it go. He says yes.
  2. Andrew pulls up in a BMW — not what Justin expected. The drive is short and easy. Andrew talks, asks questions, doesn't perform. Justin relaxes without deciding to.
  3. They arrive at a penthouse. High floor, big windows, real furniture. Justin keeps his reaction small but his internal monologue is loud. Everything he assumed about Andrew's situation was wrong.
  4. Andrew's older brother Ethan is home briefly — young, calm, clearly running something on his laptop. Not a struggling dropout. Justin recalibrates.
  5. Justin recalibrates everything: the public school transfer wasn't about money. Something else pushed Andrew out of the private school world. Justin doesn't ask.
  6. They hang out — watch something, talk about nothing. Andrew shows Justin his headphones and a playlist. It feels like being let in. Justin takes the train home. He is, quietly, in a much worse state than before.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Shock at the call → relief and excitement → disorientation at the penthouse → rapid recalibration of assumptions → comfort during the hang-out → quiet devastation on the way home
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Andrew calling instead of texting — deliberate, confident
  - "I asked around" — deliberately vague (will pay off when Marcus's POV reveals the truth)
  - The BMW and penthouse — the transfer was never about money
  - Ethan — not a struggling guardian but someone already successful
  - Face-down photo frames in Andrew's room
  - Andrew's playlist as first deliberate act of sharing
  - "See you Tuesday" — Andrew has already mapped their next encounter
- **Hook:** Justin is home and his world feels smaller. He can feel the trouble coming but doesn't know what kind.

### Chapter 9: "Invisible"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,900
- **POV:** Marcus (1st person, continued)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Marcus establishes the pattern: Andrew knows exactly who he is, but in hallways treats him like furniture. Not hostile — just absent.
  2. He tests it: walks past Andrew's locker, gets half-second eye contact then nothing. Makes a comment in Government discussion, gets a functional response then the wall drops.
  3. Brings it up with Dylan (joke), Samira ("Maybe he's just like that with some people" — Marcus finds this unsatisfying), Tyler (brief observation).
  4. At lunch, asks Justin about it. Justin instinctively defends Andrew — "His parents died, Marcus" — before his brain catches up. The group teases Justin for the defensiveness. Marcus files away the speed of Justin's reaction.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Mild annoyance → deliberate testing → dissatisfaction with explanations → recognition of Justin's subconscious investment in Andrew
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Andrew's selective blindness as deliberate compartmentalization
  - Samira reading Marcus the way Marcus reads everyone else
  - Justin's reflexive defense — body ahead of brain, mirroring his own pattern with Andrew in earlier chapters
  - Marcus now watching two things: Andrew's behavior and Justin's reaction
- **Hook:** Marcus files Justin's instinctive defense as evidence. He's now tracking both Andrew and Justin.

### Chapter 10: "Deterioration"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,700
- **POV:** Marcus (1st person, continued)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Justin and Andrew have gone silent. Justin stops mentioning Andrew. The group doesn't push it.
  2. Marcus observes Justin operating at "ninety-five percent speed" — fine on the surface, but the timing is off. Andrew in Government has dropped from seventy to sixty percent — the trailed-off sentences have stopped entirely.
  3. Marcus's private read: Andrew showed his real self, the warmth was temporary/strategic, and Justin found something he didn't expect.
  4. The secret Marcus keeps: he's developed feelings for Andrew — illogical, unexplainable, kept completely to himself.
  5. The friend group goes quiet around Andrew's name. Samira notices. Nobody says anything.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Observation → pattern recognition → private analysis → self-confrontation (the feelings) → resigned containment
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Justin at ninety-five percent — Marcus can hear the difference because he knows the original
  - Marcus's private read may be wrong — dramatic irony layer
  - Marcus's feelings described as "quieter and more dangerous" — mirrors Justin's unnamed feelings
  - Samira's look — she sees the same gap, maintains her silent-observer role
  - Marcus keeping this from Justin specifically — creates the fault line for Ch. 12
- **Hook:** The group moves around Andrew's absence like missing furniture. Marcus carries his own secret alongside Justin's.

### Chapter 11: "Not You, Not Him"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~2,000
- **POV:** Marcus (1st person, continued)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Marcus runs out of patience. He replays the contrast: Andrew's warmth toward Justin vs. blankness toward him. Starts wondering if Andrew's interest in Justin was romantic.
  2. Asks around quietly — Dylan, Becca — gathering without admitting what he's looking for.
  3. In Government class, Marcus pushes: asks Andrew directly what his problem is.
  4. Andrew responds factually: he's not interested, not in Marcus, not in Justin either. He has a partner — someone from his old school.
  5. Short conversation. Andrew isn't cruel, just final. Marcus processes alone.
  6. Doesn't tell anyone. Sits in the dark that night with the deflated feeling. Tells himself he's fine.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Frustration → directed inquiry → confrontation → absorption of rejection → private grief → forced acceptance
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Andrew has a partner from his old school — connects to Westbrook Prep backstory
  - "Justin is a different situation" — Andrew refuses to elaborate, but the phrasing implies Justin meant something
  - Andrew's deliberate distance from Marcus was calculated — he sensed the interest
  - Marcus's "fine" echoes Justin's three "fines" from Ch. 7 — parallel structure
  - Andrew's tiredness — "close to tired" — he's closed doors before
  - Marcus not telling Justin — compound secret (phone number + feelings)
- **Hook:** Marcus lies in the dark and waits for the feeling to pass. The reader knows it won't pass fast enough.

### Chapter 12: "First Punch"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~1,200
- **POV:** Marcus (1st person — ending cliffhanger)
- **Core Events:**
  1. Regular Thursday lunch. Table at its usual low hum. Marcus quieter than normal. Andrew not mentioned.
  2. Samira says something small to Marcus. He half-answers. He's thinking about the shape of everything that's changed.
  3. His name — shouted across the cafeteria. The whole table looks up.

- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Justin's eyes "wet, not crying" — the punch cost him something; this is overflow, not rage

  - The cafeteria as public stage — connects to Instagram gossip ecosystem
  - Marcus on the floor looking up — complete inversion of his usual analytical vantage point
  - The "concentric circles" of dying conversations — the whole school now has a story
- **Hook:** Maximum cliffhanger. Marcus is on the floor. Justin is standing over him. Neither moves. No explanation yet.

### Chapter 13: "The Gala"
- **Est. Word Count:** ~3,500
- **POV:** Zack (1st person) — NEW POV character
- **Structure:** 插叙 (intercut) — opens on the crisis, reaches backward into the "before" through fragments triggered by chaos
- **Core Events:**
  1. Zack establishes himself — a student from Westbrook Prep who knew Andrew by face and reputation but never by conversation. Andrew was the quiet one.
  2. *[Intercut]* The car ride: Zack's mother's precision (polished shoes as wordless instruction), father's silent rituals. The Chen family dynamic — control as love.
  3. *[Intercut]* Arriving at the venue: chandeliers, the tradition of "two hours, maybe three," existing politely.
  4. The crisis: gunshots, explosion. Zack loses his parents in the surge. Smoke, blocked exits, crowd compression.
  5. *[Intercut]* Andrew's parents at the table: the father's private half-smile, the mother's real laugh, her hand on Andrew's arm. "They looked permanent."
  6. Zack finds Andrew pinned under a collapsed column and pulls him out. They're both trapped — exits blocked, Zack's leg badly injured.
  7. Andrew calls Ethan. The brother's controlled voice. "They're fine." Zack says nothing.
- **Micro-psychological shifts:** Detached familiarity → shock of violence → intercut warmth of the "before" → adrenaline-fueled rescue → forced calm → quiet dread at Ethan's managed delivery
- **Foreshadowing:**
  - Andrew's pre-reinvention self — invisible, quiet, the person nobody wanted around
  - Ethan's controlled voice — managing terrible information in real time
  - Andrew's parents seen alive for the first and only time — "They looked permanent"
  - Zack's family dynamics: mother = precision, father = silence. The Chen way of expressing love
  - The chandeliers described as "heavy" — structural foreshadowing
  - Zack's leg injury — will keep them immobile for Ch. 14
- **Hook:** Zack sits in the dark, letting Andrew believe the lie, holding the weight of what he suspects while thinking about his own parents.

### Chapter 1: "The Transfer Kid"
**Summary:** Justin's Instagram group chat explodes with news that a private school student is transferring to West Valley High after losing both parents. Reactions range from sympathy to hostility about the "private school" label. Justin stays indifferent. On the first day, Andrew walks the hallway with a self-possession that reads as arrogance — he doesn't react to the stares, the whispers, or the mockery from the football guys. Justin watches and finds himself unsettled but can't articulate why. He's then called to Principal Nakamura's office and asked to "look after" Andrew — show him around, check in. Justin is annoyed but agrees. At lunch, he vents to Dylan, Samira, and Tyler about the absurdity of being assigned to babysit an all-AP student when he barely manages C-pluses. His friends joke, but Justin leaves the conversation with a nagging sense that Andrew is something he can't easily file away.

### Chapter 2: "Group Work"
**Summary:** Justin checks his schedule and discovers he shares only one class with Andrew — World History. He feels relieved. When Mr. Reeves assigns group work, Justin forces himself to approach Andrew and suggest they pair up, driven by his promise to Nakamura. The introduction is painfully flat: Andrew says "sure" and nothing more. During the work period, Andrew's intellectual speed is immediately apparent — he processes information effortlessly while Justin struggles to keep up, reduced to making a title slide. Justin keeps searching for any emotional crack in Andrew's surface and finds nothing. Then, at the bell, Justin suggests meeting after school to finish. Andrew refuses and tells Justin to handle the slides himself. Justin protests — he's a C-plus student who contributed nothing. Andrew looks at him, really looks for the first time, and says "I trust you." He leaves without another word. Justin is left confused: after forty-five minutes of complete blankness, three unexpected words crack the wall open an inch.
