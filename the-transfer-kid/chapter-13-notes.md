# Chapter 13: The Gala — Notes

## 1. Pre-Flight Planning
- **Core Event:** Zack introduces himself — a student from Andrew's old private school (Westbrook Prep). He knew Andrew by face and reputation but never by conversation. Andrew was the quiet one. At a charity gala, Zack sees Andrew with two adults (his parents). Then: gunfire, an explosion. The room breaks apart. Zack loses his parents in the chaos. He finds Andrew pinned under a collapsed structural column and pulls him out. They're both trapped — exits blocked, air bad, Zack's leg injured. Andrew calls Ethan; the brother says their parents are fine. Zack says nothing.
- **Emotional State Transition:** Zack starts from detached familiarity → shock of violence → adrenaline-fueled rescue → forced calm of being trapped → a quiet dread when Andrew gets false reassurance about his parents
- **Timeline Budget:** FLASHBACK. D-90 (June). The gala happens during the summer, before Andrew's transfer.
- **Supporting Cast:** Zack's parents (background), Andrew's parents (first/last appearance), Ethan (phone call only)
- **POV:** Zack (1st person) — NEW POV character.
- **Structure Note:** Uses 插叙 (intercut/flashback) technique — crisis moments trigger memories of the "before" (car ride, arriving at the venue, watching Andrew's parents, Zack's family dynamics). The contrast between the polished normalcy and the chaos carries the emotional weight.

## 2. Post-Flight Overview
- **Actual Events:** Zack establishes his Westbrook Prep background and describes Andrew as "the quiet one." The chapter opens with the crisis (gunshots) and intercuts backward to the car ride with his parents — his mother's precision, his father's silent rituals, the polished shoes, the suit that fits too well. These fragments of normalcy are triggered by crisis details: the chandeliers swaying remind him of arriving; losing his parents in the crowd triggers the memory of his father's quarter-second nod across the room; seeing Andrew's parents mentioned triggers a full intercut of watching them at the table — the father's private half-smile, the mother's real laugh, her hand touching Andrew's arm. Zack pulls Andrew from under a collapsed column. They're trapped. Andrew calls Ethan, who tells him their parents are "fine" in a controlled voice. Zack recognizes the cadence of managed information. He lets Andrew believe it.
- **Suspense Hook:** Zack sits in the dark, shoulder to shoulder with Andrew, holding the weight of what he suspects about Andrew's parents while thinking about his own. The reader feels the dramatic irony — the "before" scenes of Andrew's parents (alive, laughing, permanent) set against the "after" silence.
- **Foreshadowing Planted:**
  - Zack's family dynamics: mother = precision/control, father = silent presence. The Chen family operates through small gestures and implied instructions — this establishes Zack's character as someone shaped by restraint
  - Andrew's parents seen in full: the father's half-smile, the mother's real laugh, the casual touch on Andrew's arm. These are the only moments they appear alive in the novel — every future mention of their death carries this image
  - "They looked permanent" — the word 'permanent' used for people about to die
  - The chandeliers described as "heavy. The kind of heavy where if one came down..." — structural foreshadowing
  - Zack's mother: "Two hours. Maybe three. We'll leave when they start repeating themselves." — the routine that was supposed to protect them
  - "Belief was the only thing either of us had" — the central mercy/lie of the chapter

## 3. Review & Logic Sync
- **Actual Timeline:** Chapter covers the evening of the gala (~D-90). Intercuts reach back to the car ride (~40 minutes before) and forward to the entrapment. Ends with Zack and Andrew trapped.
- **Timeline Conflicts:** None. Flashback chapter, no main timeline interference.
- **Character Evolution:** Zack's voice is fully established through the intercuts — the Chen family's controlled warmth, the precision-as-love dynamic with his mother, the silence-as-connection with his father. Andrew's parents are given life (the only time they appear in the novel) specifically so their death carries weight. The intercut structure mirrors how trauma works: you don't experience the crisis linearly, you keep reaching for the before.
