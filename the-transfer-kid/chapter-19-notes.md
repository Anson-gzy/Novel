## 2. Post-Flight Overview
- **Actual Events:** Justin walks and takes the train home after leaving Andrew's apartment. The sequence of events replays: the bracelet, the phone, Marcus's behavior, Andrew's revelation. At home, in the dark at 1 AM, Justin builds a coherent but wrong picture: Marcus is the partner from the old school. The math is imperfect — Marcus was at West Valley before Andrew — but Justin's emotional state isn't interested in perfect math, only in a target. He doesn't sleep. The next day at school he's operating on autopilot. At Thursday lunch, he's present but reduced (the state Marcus observed in Ch. 12). He sees Andrew crossing the cafeteria, gets up to follow, loses him at a hallway intersection. Returns to the table. Sees Marcus sitting there — steady, contained, analytical. Something shifts. Justin walks over, says Marcus's name loud enough to cut through the cafeteria noise. Marcus starts to turn. The punch lands. Marcus goes down. This is the Chapter 12 scene from the other side.
- **Suspense Hook:** The cliffhanger circuit is completed. The reader now knows the full chain: Andrew's "I have a partner" → Justin's sleepless night → wrong deduction (Marcus = partner) → failed pursuit of Andrew → redirected punch. The dramatic irony is total: the reader knows Justin is wrong about Marcus. Justin does not.
- **Foreshadowing Planted:**
  - Justin's wrong deduction creates the central dramatic irony going forward — every future interaction between Justin and Marcus will be colored by this misunderstanding
  - The failed attempt to reach Andrew → misdirected to Marcus — the punch was never about Marcus specifically, it was about the closest available target for an emotion that had no other outlet
  - "His hand was still half-closed" — echoes Ch. 12's detail exactly, confirming the punch was impulsive
  - "Eyes wet but not crying" — now fully explained: heartbreak overflow from Ch. 18's revelation
  - The chapter title "Marcus" — Justin named the chapter after the person he wrongly identified as the problem
