# Chapter 7: Marcus Talks First

*(POV: Marcus)*
I'm going to say something and I need everyone to not make it weird.

That was the plan, anyway. In my head, it was a clean delivery — I'd sit down, put my food on the table, say what I had to say, and we'd move on. No drama. No Dylan-level conspiracy theories. Just information, presented plainly, so people could do with it what they wanted.

The problem with plans like that is they require other people to cooperate.

"So," I said, dropping my tray next to Dylan. Samira was across from us, picking at a salad. Becca was two seats down, reading something on her phone. Tyler was at the end of the table, eating chips and half-watching something on his phone with one earbud in. Justin wasn't here yet — he had calculus this period, or whatever standard-level math class he was in, and he always ran late from the second floor. "Andrew Hayes asked me for Justin's number."

Dylan's fork stopped halfway to his mouth.

Samira looked up.

Becca locked her phone.

Tyler pulled out his earbud.

"And his address," I added, because if I was doing this I might as well do it completely. "He asked for both."

Silence. Three full seconds of silence, which at this table is basically a world record.

Then Dylan started.

"Wait. Wait wait wait." He put his fork down. He put both hands flat on the table, like he was bracing himself. "Andrew Hayes — the Andrew Hayes, the kid who doesn't talk to anyone, the transfer kid who's been at this school for a month and has had exactly one facial expression the entire time — came up to you. Specifically. And asked for Justin's phone number. And his address."

"That's what I said."

"When?"

"Day before yesterday. After sixth period."

"Where?"

"Hallway near the gym. He was just standing there when I walked by. Like he'd been waiting."

Dylan's eyes went wide. Not surprised-wide — excited-wide. The kind of wide that meant he was about to build a theory out of nothing and present it like gospel. I'd seen this face before. It never led anywhere good.

"This is insane," Dylan said. "This is actually insane. He asked for his number and his address? Not like, 'hey do you have Justin's contact' — he specifically asked for both?"

"Both."

"And you gave them to him?"

"I gave him the number. I didn't have his address memorized, so I looked it up and texted it to him later."

"You texted Andrew Hayes."

"I texted him an address. That's not a relationship."

"Marcus." Dylan leaned forward. "My guy. Do you understand what you're telling us right now? Andrew doesn't ask anyone for anything. He doesn't approach people. He doesn't make requests. He doesn't need things from other people — that's his whole deal. And he came up to you, in a hallway, and asked you for Justin's personal information. That's not nothing."

"I didn't say it was nothing. I said I'm telling you."

Dylan sat back, both hands behind his head now, staring at the ceiling. "Okay. Okay okay okay. So the timeline is — Andrew meets Justin in History. Andrew does the whole 'I trust you' thing. Andrew starts saying hi to people. Andrew invites Justin to his house. Andrew then goes and finds Marcus to get Justin's number and address, because he somehow didn't already have them." He looked at me. "Am I missing anything?"

"That's the sequence."

"This is a whole movie."

"I mean, the address part is a little much," Tyler said. "The number I get. But the address?"

Samira hadn't said a word.

I noticed because Samira usually talks. Not as much as Dylan, but she fills gaps. She asks questions. She makes observations that are smarter than everyone else's and then goes back to her phone like she didn't just say the sharpest thing at the table.

Right now she was just sitting there. Fork in hand, salad untouched, looking at a spot on the table between Dylan and me. Not upset. Not processing. Just — quiet. The kind of quiet that meant she'd already figured out what this meant and was several steps ahead of the rest of us.

Becca was the first one to actually think before speaking.

"Did he say why?" she asked. The question was directed at me, delivered carefully, the way Becca does things. "Like, did he give a reason? Or was it just 'give me Justin's number'?"

"He said he wanted to invite him over. That was basically it. He wasn't weird about it — he just asked. Like it was a normal thing to do."

"Is it a normal thing to do?"

"I don't know. If someone else did it, yeah. But it's Andrew, so..."

"So it's not normal."

"So it's notable."

Dylan snapped his fingers. "Notable. See? Even Marcus thinks it's notable, and Marcus doesn't think anything is notable."

"That's not true."

"Name the last thing you thought was notable."

I ignored him.

"Does Justin know?" Becca asked.

"That Andrew asked me? No. Andrew asked me not to tell him."

That landed. Becca's eyebrows went up. Dylan's mouth opened and then closed again, which might have been the first time that had ever happened. Even Samira shifted in her seat.

"Wait, why not?" Tyler said.

"He asked you not to tell Justin," Dylan repeated.

"He said he'd rather Justin didn't know he had to ask. He wanted it to seem like he already had his number."

"Why?"

I shrugged. "I didn't ask why. It wasn't my business."

"You didn't ask why."

"Do I strike you as the kind of person who follows up?"

Dylan stared at me. "You are the most frustrating person I've ever met."

"Thank you."

Becca was thinking. I could see it — the way her eyes moved like she was reading a page that wasn't there. "So he wanted it to seem natural. Like he already had Justin's number and was just — reaching out. Not like he had to go find it."

"Basically."

"That's..." She hesitated. "That's actually kind of sweet. In a weird way."

"Or it's controlling," Samira said.

First thing she'd said in five minutes. Everyone turned. She was still looking at the table, but her jaw was set in a way I recognized. Not angry. Just decided.

"It could be sweet," she continued. "Or it could be controlling. We don't know which one yet." She looked at me. "What did you think? When he asked."

I thought about it. What had I thought? Andrew had walked up to me in the hallway like he knew I was the right person to ask — not Dylan, who would've turned it into a thing; not Tyler, who would've told Justin within the hour; not Becca, who might've asked too many questions. He'd picked me because I was the one least likely to make it more than it was.

"I thought he was being practical," I said. "He wanted Justin's number. I had it. He asked."

"And the secrecy part?"

"I think he doesn't want Justin to know he had to work for it. Some people are like that."

Samira nodded — a single nod, slow, like she was filing something. "Okay."

"Okay?"

"Okay." She went back to her salad. Whatever she was thinking, she wasn't sharing it yet.

  

---

Justin showed up four minutes later.

I saw him coming from across the cafeteria — tray in hand, same walk as always, same whatever-I-don't-care expression he wore to lunch every day. Except today he looked slightly different, and it took me a second to figure out why. He looked like someone who slept in a weird position and was still carrying the shape of it. Not tired, exactly. Just off.

The table shifted. Dylan picked up the conversation he'd been having with Becca about some show, mid-sentence, at a volume that was clearly artificial. Samira went back to her phone. Tyler put his earbud back in, which was actually the most natural-looking cover any of us managed. I took a bite of my sandwich.

Justin sat down.

"What's up," he said. Not a question.

"Nothing," said Dylan, way too fast.

Justin looked at him. Then at Samira. Then at me. Then at Tyler, who was chewing chips and staring at his phone like nothing had happened, which was either genuinely relaxed or the best poker face at the table. Then at Becca, who was eating her lunch with an intensity that suggested she was afraid of eye contact.

"You guys are being weird," Justin said.

"We're not being weird."

"You went quiet when I sat down. That's weird."

"We were having a conversation that naturally ended," Dylan said, which was technically true because the conversation we'd been having had, in fact, ended — about thirty seconds before Justin arrived, specifically because he was arriving.

Justin stared at him for another second, then shrugged. "Whatever."

There was a pause. Then Becca, probably because the silence was killing her, said: "So how was the hang-out? With Andrew?"

Justin picked up his water bottle. "Fine."

"Fine?"

"Yeah. Fine. Normal. We watched something, talked a little. It was fine."

Three fines. In my experience, when someone says something is fine three times in a row, it was not fine. It was either much better or much worse than fine, and they weren't going to tell you which.

"Where does he live?" Dylan asked.

"Near the metro. Not far." Justin said it casually, but there was a weight behind it — something he was choosing not to unpack in front of us. I noticed. I didn't push.

"Cool," Dylan said.

"Yeah."

Another pause. Justin took a bite of his food, chewed, and then said — almost like a side note, almost like he was thinking out loud rather than talking to anyone specific:

"I still don't know how he got my number, by the way."

The table went quiet.

Half a second. Maybe less. The kind of silence that's only noticeable if you're paying attention, and Justin was paying attention. He looked up from his tray. His eyes moved around the table — Dylan, Samira, Becca, Tyler, me. Reading us. Waiting.

Nobody said anything.

"Did he ask one of you?" Justin said.

"I don't think so," Dylan said, and I had to give him credit — the lie was smooth. Not too fast, not too slow. Natural. Like he genuinely hadn't thought about it.

"He just called me," Justin said. "Out of nowhere. On my actual phone number. And I have no idea how he got it."

Samira shrugged, still looking at her phone. "Maybe he looked you up. There are websites that do that."

"For a sixteen-year-old's cell number?"

"I'm just saying. It's not that hard."

Justin looked at me. Right at me.

I took a bite of my sandwich and looked back at him. Calm. Neutral. Chewing. I am very good at not having an expression when I don't want one, which is a useful skill that I don't get enough credit for.

He held the look for maybe two seconds. Then he dropped it.

"Strange," he said, and went back to eating.

The table relaxed. Dylan started talking about something else — a video he'd seen, a test next period, something. Samira scrolled. Becca laughed at something Dylan said. Tyler went back to his chips and his phone, already moved on in a way the rest of us were pretending to be. The gears of the lunch table turned back to normal speed, and Justin settled into them, and the moment passed.

But I watched him for a few seconds longer than I normally would. The way he ate without really tasting anything. The way he'd said "fine" three times and meant none of them. The way he'd dropped the phone number question not because he believed our answers, but because he wasn't ready to know the real one.

Something was different about him. Not dramatic — not the kind of different that announces itself. Just a shift. Like someone had moved a piece of furniture in a room he lived in, and now he kept bumping into the empty space where it used to be.

---

Here's the thing about me and this whole situation: I don't really care.

That sounds worse than I mean it. I care about Justin — he's my friend. I'm not invested in his business, but I'm not going to let something bad happen to him if I can help it. And maybe that's the problem with what I did. When Andrew asked me for Justin's number, I didn't think about it too hard. He asked. I gave it. He said don't tell Justin. I said okay.

I'm not covering for Andrew because I like the guy. I don't know the guy. We've had one interaction, and it lasted about ninety seconds. He walked up to me in the hallway like he'd identified me as the most efficient path between himself and a phone number, confirmed that I was the person he thought I was, made his request, and left.

I said I wouldn't tell Justin, so I won't. That's not loyalty. That's just what I said I'd do. I said it, so I'll do it. If I didn't plan on keeping my word I wouldn't have given it.

But I'll say this. The fact that Andrew went to the trouble — that he found me, asked for Justin's number instead of just DMing him on Instagram, and then specifically asked me to keep it quiet — tells me something. I don't know what it tells me, exactly. But it tells me something.

And Justin walking in here today, slightly off, saying "fine" three times, not sleeping well if I had to guess — that tells me something too.

Whatever's happening between those two, it's not nothing. And whatever it turns into, I'll probably hear about it eventually, because that's what happens when you're in a friend group with Justin Herrera — everything comes out, sooner or later, whether he wants it to or not.

For now, I ate my sandwich and let it go. Not my problem to solve. Not my story to tell.

But I'm watching.
