# Chapter 26: The Long Weekend

*(Third-Person Limited — POV: Andrew & Zack)*

When they walked out of the diner, the first pale light of five in the morning was breaking through the city fog.

The streets were clear of traffic. Andrew drove his black BMW south along Highway 1. Zack sat in the passenger seat with the window cracked open an inch, letting the cool ocean air clear away the lingering exhaustion of the night.

Neither of them spoke for a long stretch. The silence inside the car felt comfortable. Andrew kept one hand on the steering wheel and rested his right hand on Zack's knee. Zack did not pull away. He reached over and laced his fingers through Andrew's.

Andrew pulled the car over at a lookout point along the bluff. To the east, the sun rose over the hills, casting a warm orange light across the water.

Andrew turned to look at Zack. The redness had faded from Zack's eyes, and his posture was relaxed for the first time in weeks.

"Still tired?" Andrew asked.

Zack leaned his head against the headrest, looking out at the water. "A little. Mostly just drained."

"Let's go home and sleep," Andrew said, turning the key in the ignition.

They arrived back at the penthouse thirty minutes later.

The fourteen-floor apartment was completely silent. Ethan had flown to San Francisco on Friday to manage a regional office issue, leaving the space empty for the weekend. Andrew drew the heavy black-out curtains in his bedroom, shutting out the morning sun.

The room fell into a quiet, warm darkness.

They dropped their shoes by the closet and climbed into the large bed. Zack closed his eyes, feeling heavy sleep settle over his limbs immediately. Andrew pulled the blanket over them and shifted closer, wrapping one arm around Zack's waist.

Zack leaned back against Andrew's chest. Listening to the steady rhythm of Andrew's breathing, he drifted off within minutes.

They slept straight through the day.

When Zack opened his eyes again, faint streaks of dusk light filtered through the edge of the curtains. The digital clock on the nightstand read six-fifteen in the evening.

Andrew was awake beside him, resting his head on one hand. His hair was messy from sleep, and his face looked rested and calm. When Zack looked up, Andrew leaned down and pressed a quiet kiss to his forehead.

"Hungry?" Andrew asked.

"Yeah," Zack said, his voice thick with sleep.

They stayed inside for the rest of the weekend.

They ignored the outside world entirely. Andrew cooked simple garlic pasta in the kitchen while Zack sat on the marble counter, handing him plates and spices. In the evening, they moved to the couch with the lights off, watching old movies under the soft glow of a single floor lamp.

Andrew pulled Zack's left leg onto his lap, using his thumb to work through the tight muscles around Zack's injured knee. Zack drank black coffee, listening to Andrew offer sharp, sarcastic commentary on the movie's plot holes.

The distance that had built up between them over the past three months vanished completely.

Zack recognized the version of Andrew he was looking at now. This was not the remote, magnetic transfer student everyone gossiped about in the hallways. This was the boy from the gala basement, someone who remembered every small detail and cared far too much to admit it easily.

By Monday morning, the bright California sun burned off the coastal fog early.

Andrew parked his BMW in the student lot at West Valley High. Zack was not at school today; he had an early appointment at the sports medicine clinic in town to get his shoulder scanned.

Andrew turned off the engine and sat in the quiet car for a moment. He touched the silver Tiffany bracelet on his left wrist. The cold metal grounded him.

He knew he had one last thing to settle today.

On Wednesday night of the previous week, Justin had come to his apartment. Justin had brought a rare hardcover book he had spent weeks hunting down, complete with a small handwritten note inside. That night, amidst confusion and bad timing, things had turned physical. But when Justin tried to ask what came next, Andrew had pulled back and said the words: *I have a partner.*

Justin had left the penthouse pale and silent. The book still sat inside the front pocket of Andrew's backpack.

Andrew stared at the dark fabric of his bag. A heavy knot tightened in his stomach.

He knew he had handled things badly. In his effort to prove he could handle public school and act like a normal person, he had drawn Justin into something unfair. Justin’s feelings had been honest, which made the whole thing worse.

Andrew picked up his bag and stepped out of the car. He walked toward the main building, deciding to find Justin during homeroom to return the book and establish clear, permanent boundaries.

The energy inside the school felt strange and tense.

After third period, Andrew walked down the main corridor toward the history wing. Near the lockers, he caught a glimpse of Justin's back.

Justin looked terrible. His eyes were sunken, his shoulders were hunched, and he moved with a nervous, erratic speed. He looked as if he had not slept in days. He was moving so fast that he failed to notice Andrew standing twenty feet away.

Before Andrew could call his name, Justin turned a sharp corner and headed straight toward the central cafeteria.

Andrew frowned and followed him.

At twelve-fifteen, the cafeteria was packed and noisy.

Plates rattled, voices bounced off the high ceiling, and laughing students crowded around the long tables.

Dylan, Samira, and Becca sat at their usual spot near the center of the room. Marcus sat beside them, quietly eating a salad while checking his notes. His face was calm, though he had been quieter than usual all morning.

Justin stood near the brick pillar at the entrance.

His breathing was fast and shallow. For three days, ever since leaving Andrew's apartment on Wednesday night, his brain had been running the same sequence over and over.

The silver bracelet. The hidden phone calls. Andrew's admission of a partner from his old life. And Marcus—Marcus asking weird questions, Marcus trying to get Andrew's phone number, and the group going silent whenever the topic came up.

Driven by exhaustion and heartbreak, Justin's brain had arrived at a conclusion that felt solid to him: Marcus was the partner. Marcus had been keeping a secret with Andrew behind everyone's back.

The logic was completely wrong, but in Justin's mind, it was the only piece that fit the puzzle.

Justin scanned the crowded room and saw Andrew enter through the far doors. He tried to move toward him, but a group of varsity soccer players blocked the aisle. By the time the crowd cleared, Andrew had disappeared down the side hall.

Justin turned back around.

His eyes locked onto Marcus sitting at the middle table.

Marcus looked steady, relaxed, and completely undisturbed by the noise around him. To Justin, that calm face looked like a deliberate lie.

Justin stopped thinking. Whatever caution he usually maintained dissolved in an instant.

"Marcus!"

The shout cut through the cafeteria noise like a blade.

The tone was so raw and loud that half the tables in the room stopped talking at once.

Marcus blinked. He heard his name and began to turn his head toward the aisle.

A shadow fell over the table.

Justin sprinted the last ten feet. His jaw was set, his eyes were bloodshot, and he threw his entire weight behind a right hook that slammed directly into the side of Marcus's jaw.

A heavy crack echoed through the space.

The impact lifted Marcus sideways off his seat. He crashed hard onto the linoleum floor, knocking his plastic tray, salad, and water bottle across the ground with a loud metallic rattle.

The cafeteria went dead silent.

Students at nearby tables scrambled backward, knocking over chairs in panic.

Marcus lay on the floor, a sharp, searing pain radiating through his jaw. He pressed his hand to his face and looked up in complete shock.

Justin stood over him, breathing in heavy, ragged gasps. His right fist was still half-closed, the knuckles red from the blow. His eyes were wet, but there were no tears falling down his cheeks. He looked hollowed out, as if the punch had drained the last bit of energy he had left.

At the entrance of the cafeteria, Andrew froze.

He held the strap of his backpack tightly, the rare book still resting inside the front pocket. He looked at Marcus on the ground, then at Justin standing above him with shaking hands.

Every separate piece of the puzzle—Marcus's quiet questions, Zack's return, Justin's heartbreaking misunderstanding, and Andrew's own mistakes—collided together in a single, silent moment.

Nobody moved. The cafeteria held its breath under the harsh fluorescent lights.
