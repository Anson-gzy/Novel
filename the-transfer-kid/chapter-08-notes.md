# Chapter 8 Notes

## Chapter Overview
- **Core Event:** Andrew joins Marcus's Government class mid-semester; Marcus observes a quieter, less performative version of Andrew — one who trails off mid-sentence, stops editing too late, and operates at seventy percent without Justin in the room. Marcus identifies two distinct versions of Andrew and doesn't know which one is real.
- **Carries from Previous:** Marcus's observer position established in Ch. 7; the secret he's holding about the phone number; his "not my problem" stance being tested by proximity; Tyler's established presence in the group.
- **Hook:** Marcus decides it isn't his problem — but he keeps watching. The final line: "You look until you do."

## Chapter Notes
- **Hook:** Marcus's grudging fascination. He doesn't care about Andrew personally, but he can't stop observing the disparity between Andrew's public and private selves. The reader now has a view of Andrew that Justin doesn't, creating dramatic irony.
- **Next chapter preview:** TBD (Chapter 9 brief pending)
- **Foreshadowing planted:**
  - Andrew at seventy percent — his confidence dial adjusts based on audience. Around Justin, it's one setting; in Government without Justin, it's another; in the hallway with Mia, it's full power. This raises the question: which version is the real one, and which is Justin getting?
  - Andrew's trailed-off sentences — "oversight versus trust," "who decides what people deserve," "the right to reinvent yourself" — each one is about his own situation, disguised as classwork. Marcus catches it; nobody else does.
  - "The right to not have your past follow you" — strongest hint yet about what happened at Westbrook Prep
  - Marcus's preference for people who say what they mean — will put him in direct friction with Andrew eventually
  - Dylan adding information to his mental file — Dylan is building a theory across multiple sources (Justin's account, Marcus's observations) and will eventually synthesize something
  - Samira's absent but implied agreement — Marcus imagines her nodding, reinforcing that the perceptive members of the group are converging on the same conclusion
  - Tyler's accidental insight — his song-lyric comparison cuts through Dylan and Marcus's more analytical approach and lands surprisingly well. Marcus finds Tyler's honesty-by-accident entertaining, a mirror to his own honesty-on-purpose. Tyler also calls out Marcus's involvement ("you literally gave him Justin's number") in a way nobody else would.
  - Marcus-Tyler dynamic — Marcus sees Tyler as the only other non-performer in the group, establishing a quiet kinship that could become important later
  - Andrew's interaction with Mia — the public magnetism at full power, confirming that what he does around people is deliberate calibration, not accidental charm
  - Marcus as unreliable in his claims of indifference — he says he doesn't care three times. He's clearly paying attention in a way that contradicts this.
  - Mr. Okoro (new teacher character) — Government class setting expands the school world beyond History/Reeves
