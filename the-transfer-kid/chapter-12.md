# Chapter 12: First Punch

*(POV: Marcus)*
Thursday lunch. Second week of December.

The table was at its usual low hum — the frequency you get when everyone's present but nobody has anything urgent to say. Dylan was talking about a movie he'd seen over the weekend, delivering the plot summary like a closing argument, pausing at the dramatic beats for effect. Becca had her phone out, scrolling with one hand and eating a granola bar with the other, occasionally looking up to contribute a reaction that was perfectly timed and perfectly minimal. Tyler was across from me, working through a bag of chips with the steady rhythm of someone who treated eating as a background process.

Samira was next to me. Quiet today. Not the sharp quiet where she was thinking — just regular quiet. The kind where she was here because this was where she sat, and she'd talk when something worth talking about came up.

Justin was on my other side. Also quiet, but a different kind.

He'd been like this for a week. Maybe longer. Present but not participating — like someone who'd shown up to a party they'd already left emotionally. He ate. He nodded at the right moments. He laughed when Dylan hit a punchline, but the laugh was half a beat late, the way laughter sounds when it's a social response rather than an involuntary one.

Nobody pushed it. We'd all learned, through some collective instinct, that whatever was going on with Justin was not the kind of thing you solved by asking about it. You just made space. You kept the table running. You let him be there without requiring him to perform being there.

I was quieter than normal too. I had my own reasons, and they lived in a place I wasn't planning to visit at lunch. The conversation drifted. Dylan moved from the movie to a test he had next period. Becca said something about a post she'd seen. Tyler asked me a question about Government homework and I answered on autopilot.

Andrew was not mentioned. Andrew had not been mentioned at this table in over a week. The name had become a gap in our vocabulary — a word we all knew but none of us used, like a door everyone walked around without acknowledging it was there.

---

Samira said something to me.

Something small — I don't remember the exact words. A comment about a class, maybe, or a question about an assignment. The kind of thing that didn't require much and existed mostly to keep the social fabric from going completely slack.

I half-answered. A word, maybe two. Enough to register that I'd heard her, not enough to build a conversation on. She accepted it and went back to her lunch.

I was thinking about something else.

Not Andrew. Not exactly. More like the shape of the last few weeks — the architecture of everything that had changed since October. The way the group had shifted, slowly and then all at once, around an absence none of us had chosen. I was thinking about how strange it was that one person who wasn't even part of our friend group had managed to alter the internal dynamics of it, without trying, without being present, just by existing in the spaces adjacent to ours and then leaving.

I was thinking about Justin. About the specific flavor of his quiet — not angry, not sad, just reduced. Like someone had turned down his volume and he hadn't figured out how to turn it back up.

I was thinking about the deflated thing in my own chest, which was still there, smaller now but persistent, like a bruise you stop noticing until you press on it.

I was mostly just there. Holding my tray. Present enough. Not paying attention to who was walking across the cafeteria.

There was a sound beside me. Small and forgettable — the dull scrape of chair legs on tile, the shift of weight, a tray settling. The kind of sound that doesn't ask you to look up, so I didn't. It went straight into the background noise where everything else was already living.

I don't know how long I stayed like that.

When I came back to the surface, the space to my right felt different. Not empty, exactly — just rearranged. I registered it the way you register a change in light when someone walks past a window: delayed, peripheral, not quite real until it is.

---

My name.

Someone said my name. Or shouted it — shouted it, actually, in a way that cut through the noise of the room like a knife through paper. Not the way someone calls you to get your attention. The way someone says your name when they've been carrying it in their mouth like something heavy and they've finally decided to let it go.

The whole table looked up at the same time. Dylan's sentence stopped mid-word. Becca's phone went down. Tyler's hand froze halfway into his chip bag. Samira turned. Justin —

Justin.

He was standing ten feet away. He'd pushed back from the table at some point in the last few seconds — or had he been somewhere else? I couldn't reconstruct it. He was just there, standing, and the look on his face was something I had never seen before. Not anger, exactly. Something rawer than anger. Something that hadn't been processed through any of the filters Justin usually ran his emotions through before they reached the surface.

His hands were at his sides. His jaw was set. His eyes were locked on me with a focus that felt physical — like being pinned to the chair by someone's stare.

"Marcus," he said again. Quieter this time, but the cafeteria had already gone half-silent around us, conversations dying in concentric circles from whatever had just happened to whatever was about to happen, so the quiet version carried just as far.

I turned.

I got about halfway through the motion — chair swiveling, shoulders coming around, my mouth opening to say something, probably "what" — before the punch landed.

It caught the side of my jaw. Not clean — slightly off-center, knuckles connecting with the bone below my ear rather than the flat of my chin. But hard. Harder than I would have expected from Justin, who had never hit anyone as far as I knew, who settled things with words and avoidance and the kind of tactical retreat that looked like indifference.

This was not indifference.

The impact turned my head. The tray went. My elbow hit the bench and my weight followed it and then I was on the floor — not fallen, exactly, but down, suddenly and completely, the linoleum cold against my palm and the noise of the cafeteria rushing back in like someone had unmuted the world.

I heard Samira say something. I heard Dylan's chair scrape. I heard the specific, unmistakable sound of a cafeteria full of teenagers collectively realizing that something real had just happened.

I looked up from the floor.

Justin was standing over me. His hand was still half-closed. His chest was moving — not panting, just breathing, the kind of deep, deliberate breathing that happens when your body does something your brain hasn't caught up to yet.

His eyes were wet. Not crying — just wet. Like the punch had cost him something, too.

He didn't say anything else. He just stood there, looking down at me, and I looked up at him, and the cafeteria held its breath, and neither of us moved.
