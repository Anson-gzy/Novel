# Chapter 24: 2 AM

*(POV: Zack)*

Neither of us moved for a while after he said it.

His knee was still against mine. The music was still playing — ambient, no words, just slow waves of sound that matched the rhythm of the ocean I could hear through the glass. The terrace lights threw long shadows across the living room floor. Everything felt still.

"How long have you known?" I said.

"Known is a strong word. Suspected. Since Tuesday."

"What gave it away?"

He tipped his head back against the couch. Looked at the ceiling. "Little things. The way you talk about food — you said 'acidic enough to cut the fat' about a bowl of ramen. That's not normal seventeen-year-old vocabulary. That's someone who's eaten at restaurants where people talk like that."

"Could just be a food nerd."

"You also said 'brine' instead of 'salt water' when you were talking about swimming. And you described your friend's car as 'aspirational' — which is a word people use when they're describing something below their own standard, not above it."

I exhaled. He'd been tracking these things. Filing them away. Building a case without ever pressing it, just letting the evidence accumulate on its own schedule.

"You're observant," I said.

"I make videos for a living. Observation is literally the job."

"Okay. So you think I'm — what? Lying about who I am?"

"I think you're telling me a version that's simpler than the real one. I think the real version involves more money than a Honda Civic and a school that's probably not in the Valley." He paused. "But like I said. I don't care."

"Why not?"

He turned his head and looked at me. Close. We were close enough that I could see the different colors in his eyes — not uniform brown but layered, darker at the edges.

"Because the person who's been texting me all week is real. I can tell. The details might be wrong but the person is right. And I'd rather talk to a real person with a fake name than a fake person with a real one." He shrugged. "I've met a lot of the second kind."

I let that sit. My chest was doing something — not tight, not loose. Something in between. Like a hinge that had been stuck for a long time and was starting to move.

"I'm still going to call you Justin," he said.

"I know."

"Unless you want to tell me your actual name."

"Not tonight."

"Okay. Not tonight."

---

The wine was gone. He'd opened a second bottle somewhere between the couch and the conversation about names, and that was gone too. I wasn't drunk — I could feel the edges of the alcohol in my limbs, a warmth in my face, a looseness in my shoulders, but my head was clear. The kind of clear that comes after you've been tense for a while and then stop.

"Tell me about Moon," I said.

Wade was lying on his end of the couch now, legs stretched out, feet almost touching my thigh. He had his phone in one hand but wasn't looking at it. Just holding it, the way some people hold a pen or a coin — something to occupy the fingers while the brain does other things.

"What do you want to know?"

"The 'not traditional' part. You mentioned it. I'm curious."

He set the phone down on his chest. "Moon and I have been together for two years. He's a model — editorial, runway, the whole thing. He travels. A lot. He's in New York right now for a shoot. He'll be back Sunday."

"And you're okay with that. Him being gone."

"I'm okay with that."

"And the other thing."

He looked at me. "What other thing?"

"You invited a stranger to your house on a Friday night while your boyfriend is in New York. That's either very trusting or very deliberate."

He laughed. Short, genuine, through his nose. "Both. Moon knows I talk to people. He talks to people. We've been doing this long enough that the rules aren't really rules anymore — they're more like agreements. We agree to be honest. We agree to not make it weird. We agree that a person can be important to you without threatening what you already have."

"And does that work?"

"Most days. Some days it's harder than others. But the alternative — pretending you're only attracted to one person for the rest of your life — that's a bigger lie than anything you've told me this week."

He said it without weight. Like reporting the temperature. This is what it is. This is how it works. Take it or leave it.

I took it.

"Does Moon know about me?" I said.

"He knows I've been texting someone. He doesn't know you're here. I'll tell him tomorrow. He won't care."

"You're sure?"

"I'm sure. Moon's thing is honesty, not exclusivity. As long as I don't hide things, he doesn't have a problem."

"That's — unusual."

"Yeah. Most people think so. Most people also stay in relationships where they're miserable because they're more afraid of being unusual than being unhappy. I'd rather be unusual."

I looked at him. He was still lying back, relaxed, his body taking up space the way confident people do — not performing it, just inhabiting it. Everything about him was direct. No filters, no performance, no careful calibration of what to show and what to hide.

Andrew was the opposite. Andrew was walls and cracks and calibration. Everything deliberate, everything weighed, every word placed with architectural precision. Being around Andrew was like standing in a building that was beautiful but engineered — you admired it, but you never forgot it was constructed.

Being around Wade was like being outside. Open air. Whatever the weather was, it was honest weather.

---

"Can I ask you something?" I said.

"You keep asking if you can ask things. Just ask."

"Why me? You have a million followers. People in your DMs constantly. Why respond to a blank profile with no picture?"

He sat up. Pulled his legs in. We were facing each other now, close, the space between us reduced to a few inches.

"Because you were funny without trying to be. That's rare. Most people in my DMs are either flirting or selling something. You were just — you. Whoever you actually are. And the Swedish furniture line was genuinely good."

He reached over and took my wine glass out of my hand. Set it on the floor next to his. Slow. Deliberate. The kind of motion that gives the other person time to object.

I didn't object.

"Also," he said, quieter now. "You looked at my content like it meant something. Not the performance. The actual thing underneath it. Nobody does that."

He leaned forward.

The distance between us closed. Not fast, not slow. Just the natural pace of two people who have been orbiting the same point for five days and finally letting the orbit decay.

His hand came up and rested on the side of my neck. Warm. His thumb was on my jaw. My pulse was going — not fast, just present, the way you feel your heartbeat in your throat when you're about to dive.

"Don't you have a boyfriend?" I said.

His mouth was close enough that I could feel the shape of his answer before he said it.

"You already know the answer to that."

---

It was different from Andrew.

I didn't mean to compare. The thought came anyway, the way thoughts come at inconvenient times — unwanted, accurate, impossible to ignore.

With Andrew, everything had been dark and slow and layered with meaning. Every touch had carried the weight of what we'd been through together — the gala, the rubble, the day and night, the silence. Touching Andrew was like touching something that had survived a fire. You were careful. You moved slowly. You treated every surface like it might still be hot.

Wade wasn't careful. Wade was immediate. He kissed the way he talked — direct, physical, no preamble. His hands were sure. He knew where he wanted to be and got there without hesitation, without checking in with his face for permission, without the careful choreography that Andrew used to manage every interaction.

I responded the same way. No thinking. No calculating. Just the physical fact of another person's body against mine, the specific temperature of his skin, the smell of wine and lime and something that was just him.

We moved from the couch to the bedroom without discussion. He walked backward, pulling me by the front of my shirt. The hallway was dark. His room was cold — a window open, ocean air, the sound of waves.

His bed was large. White sheets. The window faced the water.

He pulled his hoodie off in one motion. Underneath, his body was lean — swimmer-adjacent, the kind of build you get from surfing or climbing or existing in a way that involves regular physical engagement with the world. I pulled my shirt over my head.

He looked at me. Really looked — the flat assessment of someone who's processing visual information without any impulse to pretend they're not. His eyes moved across my shoulders, my chest, the lines where years of swimming had carved specific definitions that normal seventeen-year-olds didn't have.

"Yeah," he said. "You're not a decent swimmer."

I almost laughed.

What happened next was unhurried. It was specific. It was physical in a way that left no room for the part of my brain that wanted to analyze, compare, or feel guilty. Bodies don't lie, and bodies don't perform — they respond, and the responses are honest, and for the first time in weeks I was somewhere where honesty didn't hurt.

His mouth on my collarbone. My hand on the small of his back. The weight of him shifting above me, then beside me, then against me. The sound of his breathing changing — faster, less controlled, the measured version of himself dropping away layer by layer until there was nothing left but the raw physical fact of two people in a cold room with an open window and the ocean doing its thing outside.

Afterward.

We lay there. Not touching, then touching — his leg against mine, his arm draped across my chest, his fingers tracing slow circles on my shoulder. The circles had no pattern. Just motion. The kind of idle, absent gesture that means someone is comfortable enough with your body to forget they're touching it.

The room smelled like salt air and skin. The curtains moved with the breeze. Neither of us said anything.

There was nothing to say. No promises to make, no terms to negotiate, no relationship to define. Just this. Just now. The specific coordinates of this bed, this room, this person, this hour. Two in the morning on a Friday in November with the Pacific Ocean twelve feet below and a stranger's hand on my shoulder, drawing circles that meant nothing and everything and nothing again.

I closed my eyes.

---

I don't know how long we stayed like that. Long enough for my breathing to slow. Long enough for the circles on my shoulder to stop, then start again, then stop. Long enough for the specific, careful silence of two people who aren't sleeping but aren't talking to settle over us like weather.

Then my phone lit up.

It was on the nightstand. I'd left it there when we moved from the living room — screen-down, silent, the responsible thing to do when you're trying to exist inside a single moment without the outside world pulling you back out.

The screen glowed. A notification. I didn't reach for it. But the room was dark enough that I could read it from where I was lying.

*Instagram: andrewhayes_ started following waderichardson.*

My body went cold.

Not gradually — instantly. The way you go cold when you jump into a pool before your body has time to adjust. My shoulders locked. My breathing stopped mid-exhale. I stared at the words on the screen and read them again and again, each time hoping they'd rearrange themselves into something different, something that didn't mean what they obviously meant.

Andrew. Andrew followed Wade. Andrew, who was on my real Instagram follow list. Andrew, who existed in the Zack part of my life, the real part, the part I'd locked away behind a fake name and a blank profile. Andrew had somehow found Wade — through the algorithm, through a search, through whatever invisible thread connected people in the digital ecosystem — and followed him.

Which meant that if Wade looked at his new followers, and if he then looked at Andrew's profile, and if Andrew's profile had any trace of me — a tagged photo, a comment, a mutual follow — the wall between Justin and Zack would crack.

Wade's hand was still on my shoulder. His breathing was steady. He hadn't seen the screen.

I lay perfectly still. The ocean sounded the same. The curtains moved the same. Everything in the room was exactly as it had been thirty seconds ago, and everything outside the room had just shifted in a way I couldn't control and couldn't take back.

My two worlds had touched.

And I was lying between them, naked, with a name that didn't belong to me and a phone full of evidence that it never had.
