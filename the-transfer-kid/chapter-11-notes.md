# Chapter 11 Notes

## Chapter Overview
- **Core Event:** Marcus confronts Andrew directly in Government class, asking why Andrew ignores him. Andrew responds plainly: he's not interested, he has a partner from his old school, and he's not interested in Justin either. Marcus absorbs the rejection alone without telling anyone.
- **Carries from Previous:** Weeks of being invisible; Marcus's growing feelings; the contrast between Andrew's warmth toward Justin and blankness toward Marcus; the friend group's silence on the topic.
- **Hook:** Marcus sits in the dark with the deflated feeling and waits for it to pass. The reader knows he'll carry this into the next chapter with no outlet.

## Chapter Notes
- **Hook:** The devastating quietness of Marcus's processing — no outburst, no confession, just a light turned off and a person waiting in the dark.
- **Next chapter preview:** The cafeteria punch — whatever Justin found out (about Marcus's feelings? about Andrew? about something else?) reaches critical mass.
- **Foreshadowing planted:**
  - Andrew has a partner — major reveal. "Someone from his old school" — this connects to the Westbrook Prep backstory and raises new questions about why he transferred
  - "Justin is a different situation" — Andrew refuses to elaborate, but the phrasing implies Justin *was* something to him, even if not romantic
  - Andrew's read on Marcus was correct all along — he sensed Marcus's interest and was maintaining distance on purpose, not through obliviousness
  - Marcus's confrontation style — "I don't ease into it. I don't do that." — consistent with his established character
  - The deflated thing — "something I hadn't realized was still inflated" — Marcus's self-awareness has limits; his feelings grew beyond his own surveillance
  - Marcus not telling Justin — this secret, layered on top of the phone number secret from Ch. 7, creates a compound betrayal that Justin may eventually discover
  - "I was fine. I'd been fine before." — the repetition of "fine" echoes Justin's three "fines" in Ch. 7, creating a mirror between the two characters
  - Andrew's tiredness — "something close to tired" — suggests this conversation is not new for him; he's had to close doors before
